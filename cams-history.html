CAMS History

Atlas, Blind Prophet and Primer: read 48 societies on the CAMS instruments, then call the crisis before the year arrives.

Open the Atlas Play Blind Prophet Read the Primer

What it is

CAMS History is an interactive way into the JUNO panel: 48 societies scored year by year on eight institutional nodes (Helm, Shield, Lore, Stewards, Craft, Hands, Archive, Flow) and four dimensions (Coherence, Capacity, Stress, Abstraction). It has three screens. The first lets you browse the record, the second tests whether you can read it without the names, and the third explains the instrument.

It was built with Grok (xAI) in November 2026 and runs entirely in your browser. There is no sign-in and no server behind it.

The three screens

Atlas

The societies with the names on. Pick a society from the list and scrub through its years. For each year you see the eight nodes, the node values, the bond ring and the society's mean value on a chart over time. A dashed line marks the strained floor, and red dots mark years with system-wide flags.

Blind Prophet

The same instrument with the names and the calendar covered. A window of years streams forward and you watch the eight nodes. You get two calls to say "the break is here". Afterwards you name the society and, separately, the shape of what it did (its archetype). Three rounds per game.

Primer

How to read the instrument: what the four dimensions mean, how node value is calculated, what each of the eight nodes stands for, and when a year counts as strained or severe.

How to read it

V = C + K − S + ½A

Each node's value V is its coherence (C) plus its capacity (K), minus its stress (S), plus half its abstraction (A). The app shows the mean of the eight node values for each year, and the worst of them.

  • A year is strained when the worst node is under 4.
  • A year is severe when value passes through zero.

For the full definitions of the dimensions and nodes, see Canonical Definitions.

What it is not

Blind Prophet is a teaching game, not a blinded test. The answers for each round (the society, its onset years and its archetype) are in the data file your browser downloads, so anyone who looks can see them. For the project's actual blind experiments, which keep the key away from the person or model being tested, see Results.

The panel inside the app is a copy of the JUNO data taken when the app was built. It does not update automatically when the JUNO datasets change. The live datasets are on the Datasets page.

Source and rebuilding

The source is in cams-history/ in the repository: a TanStack Start app, with the CAMS calculations in src/lib/cams/model.ts and the data in public/data/panel.json (named panel) and public/data/cases.json (blind rounds).

The site serves a prerendered static copy at cams-history/app/. After changing the app, run npm run build:static in cams-history/ and commit the rebuilt app/ folder; the site host does not run a build step.
