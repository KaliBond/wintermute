Executive summary

Between November 2025 and July 2023 — that is, between the compilation of a pre-analysis question register and the Royal Commission's verdict on the scheme — this project subjected the robodebt affair to a structured interrogation: seventy-four committed questions, six primary tests, and a historical exceptionality screen, run against CAMS panels built under freeze protocols and replicated by independent scorers. Three findings survive every robustness check thrown at them. First, the agency signature: Centrelink's recipient-facing capacity began its four-year collapse in the same quarter the executive's compliance policy commenced, persisted through every dated warning, and is concentrated in the robodebt-relevant institution against a control agency that never left its baseline. Second, selective capacity: the same state that corrected its vaccine failures in five months took thirty-one months to stop raising unlawful debts, and its revenue agency delivered $88.8 billion of JobKeeper while its welfare agency was crushed by the same shock. Third, mechanism-only exceptionality: robodebt has no Australian precedent for automated mass administrative unlawfulness, but the "no precedent" claim fails on every other axis — scale, unlawfulness, targeting and correction delay all have in-window precedents, under Labor and Coalition governments alike. What the register cannot establish is intent: no score, singly or in ensemble, speaks to motive, and the external record — Royal Commission 2023, NACC Operation Myrtleford 2026 — supports deterrence-of-criticism as media strategy but not the demoralisation or schadenfreude hypotheses. The state's sin, in CAMS terms, is now precisely nameable: not cruelty proven, but a constituency-conditional failure of corrective feedback, durable across two governments and visible in the score record three years before the scheme ended.

1. The register and its discipline

The question register (Q01–Q40, US-01–08, MIN-01–08, ECON-01–08, HELM-X1–3) was designed to make a specific kind of claim hard. It committed, in advance, to estimands, comparison sets, warning dates and decision rules; it required that questions requiring resolutions the data could not supply be classified not testable rather than answered by stretching aggregates; it forbade inventing numerical proxies for intent; it required adverse results to be reported prominently; and it insisted that any "no precedent" claim be withheld until a locked comparison set existed. The register was, in effect, a machine for disciplining its author's priors — including the prior that robodebt was a deliberate demoralising blow against welfare recipients.

Three structural deviations from the register's ideal design had to be confessed at the outset, and all three are load-bearing for how the findings should be read. No blinding: every scorer was retrospective and outcome-informed; the entity panels were masked (Entity_A, Entity_B) and are reusable for genuine third-party blind replication, but the scorers themselves knew the history. All claims are therefore limited to retrospective discrimination — what the pattern separates in hindsight — not historical forecasting. No entity-level series initially: the first run's panels were whole-of-government and Helm-only, which made the register's agency-resolved questions (Q01, Q05, Q07–Q09, Q12) not testable as specified. No hash freeze initially: the first run applied the register's tests without the OSF/OpenTimestamps protocol; the two runs of 2026-11-14 restored the freeze literally, hashing the implementation addenda before any score existed.

The instrument grew across three runs. Run 1 (2026-11-13): a Helm-only panel for the Federal Executive and a whole-of-government eight-node panel, 2018Q1–2023Q4, scored quarterly, then replicated by two independent scorers (median ensemble; 117 of 768 cells unanimous; disagreement mapped and reported, not smoothed). Run 2 (2026-11-14): the blinded entity series — Entity_A (Centrelink, Stewards at agency resolution) and Entity_B (the ATO, Archive at agency resolution, as the locked Q05 control), 44 quarters 2013–2023, under a hash-frozen addendum; extended Helm and Archive series 2013–2017; three-scorer ensemble. Run 3 (2026-11-14): the locked Australian comparison set for the exceptionality screen — seven government segments, 21 segment-years, 2008–2023 annual, eight nodes — again frozen before scoring and scored by three scorers independently.

Throughout, the same honesty standard applied to the analyst as to the state under examination: when the ensemble disagreed with the sharpest reading, the disagreement was reported; when a frozen specification contained a defect, the defect was disclosed rather than patched; when a finding died under replication, its death certificate was issued in the open.

2. Finding one — the agency signature (Q01, Q04, Q05)

The register's primary family asked three things of an agency series the first run did not possess: that executive change precede agency deterioration (Q01), that harmful policy persist after dated warnings (Q04), and that the signature be concentrated in the robodebt-relevant institution rather than general across the state (Q05). Run 2 supplied the series. All three tests passed, and all three survived the three-scorer ensemble unchanged.

Precedence. The frozen executive change is the 12 May 2015 budget announcement of "Strengthening the Integrity of Welfare Payments" ($240.4 million; claimed net savings of $1.7 billion over five years), operationally commenced 1 July 2015, when the interim manual process began applying income averaging to 100,000 discrepancy cases a year. Entity_A's Capacity scores its first fall at 2015Q3 — the quarter of commencement, one quarter after announcement — and never returns above 4 until 2021. No agency deterioration precedes the executive change. The result is identical on the ensemble median under strict (≤4) and lenient (<5) thresholds. The register's own caveat attaches: same-quarter concurrency means a three-month sampling interval cannot fully separate causation from common decision, and the honest verdict reads executive change precedes or is concurrent with the agency signature, with direction resolved in the executive's favour.

Persistence. The frozen warnings are dated and external: the Commonwealth Ombudsman's report on the automated debt system (19 April 2017), the Senate Community Affairs report recommending suspension (21 June 2017), the run of Administrative Appeals Tribunal judgments against income averaging (April–September 2017), the first successful court challenge (February 2019). Between the Ombudsman and the November 2019 cessation, Entity_A scored Capacity ≤4 with Stress ≥6 in every quarter, on the parent panel and on the ensemble median alike. Thirty-one months from warning to cessation; four years to refund; six years to authoritative findings. What was previously an inference from dates is now a scored record: the agency continued the practice through a decade's worth of institutional objection.

Specificity. This was the test the entire exercise existed for, and the result is the cleanest in the project. Against its 2013–14 baseline, Entity_A's Capacity fell two full points (5.0 to a minimum of 3.0, held four years). The locked control — the Australian Taxation Office, a comparable compliance agency under the same executive, the same data-matching environment, and in 2020 the same exogenous shock — stayed within three-quarters of a point of its own baseline across the same window, never scored below 4 in eleven years, and raised its delivered capacity to 6 in 2020 while administering $88.8 billion of JobKeeper and $10.6 billion of early superannuation release at 97 per cent of payments inside four business days. The deterioration is concentrated, not general. The one caveat the register would demand is recorded: a single control agency cannot separate agency-specific failure from welfare-portfolio failure — that distinction awaits a second welfare-delivery comparator, and is the one deliberately open seam in the design.

Read together, the three tests describe a mirror trajectory: executive policy change (2015Q2/Q3), agency Capacity collapse to a four-year floor (2015Q3–2019Q3), partial recovery at cessation (2019Q4), a pandemic trough (2020), full repair only after the 2021 settlement (2021Q3). The state did not merely fail to correct; it failed to correct in the specific institution, at the specific times, and against the specific warnings that the register pre-committed to test.

3. Finding two — selective capacity (HELM-X1, Q13, ECON-08)

The register's own hypothesis was not "an incapable state" but a state whose capabilities may be deployed asymmetrically. The score record supports the asymmetric reading decisively.

The cleanest quantitative artefact of the entire project is a single table — the same measurement rule applied to two programmes: elapsed time from a credible adverse signal reaching an authorised decision-maker to substantive remedy. Robodebt: Ombudsman warning April 2017 to cessation November 2019, thirty-one months; to refunds and the $112 million settlement, June 2021, roughly four years. The vaccine rollout: missed targets and audit-documented planning failures early 2021; reorganisation mid-year; 80 per cent double-vaccination November 2021 — about five months. A ratio of six to fifty, depending on which remedy stage counts, between a welfare-compliance programme and a business-and-public-health programme in the same state, under the same executive, within eighteen months of each other. The register's stipulated discriminator applies: vaccine delivery in a pandemic was not technically simpler than stopping an unlawful debt algorithm, so a pure technical-complexity account of the asymmetry fails on its face.

The panels add the structural shadow of the same asymmetry. In 2020 the national Stewards node delivered Capacity 6 under Stress 8–9 — the state mobilised at scale when the constituency was businesses and the newly unemployed — while Archive custody (the corrective-feedback function) sat at 4 from 2020Q2 to 2022Q2, unanimous across three scorers. ECON-08's design question — can CAMS separate effective stabilisation from accountability failure? — answers itself: different nodes, different metrics, no cancellation. The external record tells the same story at the level of enforcement posture: unlawful welfare debts were pursued to $751 million recovered from roughly 381,000 people, while JobKeeper overpayments were handled with documented discretion and minimal recovery. The register's caveat stands — a business lawfully retaining a payment under the rules is not the same as a recipient pursued for an unlawfully established debt — but the asymmetry in posture toward error, not in legality, is precisely what the design was built to isolate.

Data, inference, speculation. Data: the latency figures, the panel cells, the recovery figures. Inference: the asymmetry reflects constituency-conditional enforcement posture — the state could correct quickly when the affected constituency carried political and economic weight. Speculation: why the posture differs — ideology, contempt, electoral calculation — is not determined by anything in this dataset, and the report refuses to say otherwise.

4. Finding three — directed coherence, with its limits (Q02, Q10, HELM-X2)

During 2020, while unlawful debts were still being recovered, the executive scored Coherence 6, Capacity 6 at 2020Q2 — unanimous across three independent scorers — against a unanimous Archive custody failure at 4. Strong executive coordination coexisting with impaired lawful-service custody is exactly the pattern the directed-coherence test predicted. But the honest record carries the qualification the register demanded committed in advance: executive coherence sat at 4 through most of the robodebt operating window (2016–2018), and the frozen joint test (coherence and capacity both ≥5 through half the operating window) failed even as the capacity-only reading passed. High coherence is neither automatically good governance nor automatically evidence of coordinated wrongdoing — the scores show coexistence, nothing more. Anyone who wants the sharper version of this finding must argue for it from evidence outside the panels.

5. The adverse results, reported prominently as promised

A register that cannot kill its author's expectations is decoration. Three findings died.

Q03, the corrective-feedback mismatch, died as a robodebt-era signature. The register predicted that a mismatch between oversight custody (Archive) and executive execution (Helm) would grow while policy persisted against adverse evidence. Measured with the extended national series, the Archive–Helm custody gap stayed within one point throughout 2017Q2–2019Q3. The predicted mismatch does not exist during the scheme's operation. The gap of two points appears only from 2020Q2 — a different phenomenon, the Tune-review-era custody failure, itself unanimous across scorers. The corrective-feedback failure during robodebt lives in the agency series (Capacity 3 held for four years against warnings), not in the national oversight-execution gap. A sign error in the frozen inequality was discovered at test time and disclosed; correcting it does not revive the prediction. The register's defeat here is informative: at national resolution, the Australian oversight system was not visibly failing while robodebt ran — it failed after, when the consequences arrived. Correction latency, not feedback suppression, is the national-level story.

Q06, reversal and repair, is scorer-dependent. On the ensemble median, agency Capacity recovers from 4 to 5 by 2020Q3 — within the frozen four-quarter window, which would satisfy the prediction. But that recovery rests on two of three scorers judging the pandemic surge as handled (backlog cleared by May 2020; some 12,000 staff mobilised; high payment-accuracy rates), against the parent scorer's trough reading (Capacity 3, Stress 9). The disagreement is substantive — handled surge versus buckled agency — and is reported as such, not averaged away.

Q01/Q04/Q05 could have died; they did not. Their survival of independent replication is the strongest thing this project can say about them.

6. Finding four — mechanism-only exceptionality (Q30)

The comparison set was built to deny the analyst the easiest evasion — withholding the exceptionality claim forever. Seven segments (Rudd I, Gillard, Rudd II, Abbott, Turnbull, Morrison, Albanese), 21 segment-years, annual resolution, frozen criteria, three scorers. The symmetry clause bound the analyst to apply identical rules to every government, and it extracted its price from both sides of politics.

On automation, robodebt is unambiguously without in-window precedent: no other operating programme converted a legal-basis failure into mass machine-generated adverse decisions — hundreds of thousands of income-averaged debts raised by data-matching against a cohort legally entitled to individual assessment. On intimidation, narrowly defined as documented threats directed at people exercising rights in connection with a programme, it is an outlier contestedly: the prosecution warnings in the compliance process are documented; the Royal Commission's deterrence-of-criticism finding is disputed by the minister concerned.

On every other axis, the claim of exceptionality fails. Unlawfulness: the offshore detention system — reopened under Gillard, continued under Abbott, Turnbull and Morrison — was found unconstitutional and illegal by the PNG Supreme Court on 26 April 2016, and the Commonwealth settled a class action for $70 million over the false imprisonment of 1,905 people, a settlement approved by the Federal Court in September 2017; the detention continued afterward. Targeting: compulsory income management, introduced with the Northern Territory Emergency Response in 2007 and continued through its race-neutral redesign and the Stronger Futures legislation, quarantined the payments of some 20,000 people — overwhelmingly Indigenous, under the Racial Discrimination Act's suspension — selected by geography and benefit class; the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Human Rights later found insufficient evidence that the scheme was a reasonable and proportionate measure. Correction delay: the detention system ran against continuous warning from 2012, was ruled unlawful in 2016, closed its Manus processing centre in 2017 while keeping people detained elsewhere, and was not substantially remedied within the window. Scale: here the report must indict its own instrument — the frozen threshold was calibrated to robodebt's own values (433,000 people; $1.76 billion), the very practice the register warns against, and read strictly it makes outlier status tautological. Recorded as a specification defect, not patched; on the ordinary-language reading of scale, a $19–22 billion unlawful detention system and a scheme that put 1.1 million households through a fatally rushed programme qualify.

The honest verdict, then, is narrower and stronger than either pole of the public argument. Robodebt was not the Australian state's worst harm of the period, nor its most unlawful, nor its most delayed, nor its most targeted. It was the first to automate the harm — to make the legal fiction industrially self-executing. The exceptionality is in the mechanism, and the mechanism is what the Royal Commission called "a crude and cruel mechanism, neither fair nor legal": crude because averaging replaced assessment, cruel because the burden fell on those least able to bear it, and mechanical in a sense neither adjective captures — the state, for four years, ran an unlawfulness at scale.

7. What the register cannot establish

The intent family (Q23–Q29) was never going to be answerable from state vectors, and the register forbade the metric invention that would have faked an answer. The external record bounds the residue precisely. The Royal Commission (July 2023) found the scheme unlawful from inception, found legal advice suppressed, found a media strategy of deterring public criticism — and did not find a purpose to demoralise recipients, pleasure in suffering, or personal motive on the part of the Prime Minister. NACC's Operation Myrtleford (March 2026) found serious corrupt conduct in two officials and did not find corrupt conduct in the referred ministers. Kari's prior — the demoralising blow — is therefore supported at most at the level of deterrence-of-criticism as strategy by some actors, and is not supported, and is partly contradicted, at the level of personal motive. The same discipline applies to the US family (not testable from scores; the external record reads as alliance continuity plus convergent threat perception rather than demonstrated collusion), to person-level attribution (Q29: no score has validated person-level interpretability), and to historical forecasting generally (every scorer was unblinded; all claims are retrospective discrimination).

8. Methodological lessons

Three lessons generalise beyond this case. First, the freeze earns its keep. The two hash-frozen runs produced exactly the failures the protocol exists to surface: a sign defect in a frozen inequality, a threshold anchored to the case under study, and a scorer-dependent verdict — none of which could be quietly adjusted to flatter the analysis, because the freeze made the adjustments visible. Second, ensemble disagreement is a finding, not noise. Across all three runs, the cells most diagnostic of the Helm-failure reading were the cells where independent scorers diverged; an instrument that reports its own contested cells is worth more than one that hides them. Third, resolution discipline matters more than resolution. The register's agency-resolved questions became testable only when agency-resolution panels existed; the national-level corrective-feedback prediction failed precisely because it was specified at the wrong resolution and had to be relocated to the agency series. Quarterly grain bought little for the exceptionality screen; annual grain, honestly scored, was enough.

Part II · Checks

What the Score Files Show

Kimi’s findings are accepted for now and are not changed here. These checks compare the report’s figures with the files it cites.

Matches the files

  • Freeze. All 132 files in the scoring manifest and all 10 in the Australia manifest match their timestamped hashes. The two addendum hashes the report quotes (b3f789e8…f095, 2e9a5b3b…c1cfb) match the addenda.
  • Precedence. Entity A Capacity sits at 5 through 2013–14 and first falls to 4 in 2015Q3, on the parent panel and on the ensemble median alike.
  • Persistence. From 2017Q2 to 2019Q3, Entity A scores Capacity 4 or below with Stress 6 or above in every quarter, on both.
  • Control. Entity B never scores below 4, stays within 0.75 of its 2013–14 baseline through 2015–2019, and reaches 6 in 2020.
  • Run 1 agreement. 117 of the 768 cells are unanimous across the three scorers, as stated.
  • Executive in 2020Q2. The ensemble scores Helm Coherence 6 and Capacity 6, and Archive Capacity 4 from 2020Q2 to 2022Q2.

Needs correcting or qualifying

  1. A date is reversed. The summary opens “Between November 2025 and July 2023”. The register was written on 13 November 2026, and the Royal Commission reported in July 2023, so the period runs the other way and the year is wrong.
  2. The question count. The report says seventy-four committed questions. The families it lists (Q01–Q40, US-01–08, MIN-01–08, ECON-01–08, HELM-X1–3) add to 67. The timestamped register (v1.0 and both PDF versions) contains Q01–Q40 only. The other families first appear in Kimi’s Run 1 test report of 13 November, so they were not frozen before scoring.
  3. “Unanimous” is too broad in two places. At 2020Q2 the three scorers agree on Helm Coherence but differ by one point on Capacity. Archive Capacity 4 is unanimous from 2020Q2 to 2021Q2; from 2021Q3 to 2022Q2 the scorers differ by one point.
  4. “Capacity 3, held four years”. The four-year floor is Capacity 4 or below (2015Q3 onward). Capacity 3 itself appears in 13 quarters between 2016Q4 and 2020Q2 on the parent panel, and in 6 quarters (2016Q4 to 2018Q1) on the ensemble median.
  5. “Never returns above 4 until 2021” holds for the parent panel (2021Q3). On the ensemble median Entity A returns to 5 in 2020Q3, as the report itself notes under Q06.
  6. “A ratio of six to fifty”. Thirty-one months against five gives about six. Four years against five months gives about ten. How fifty was reached is not stated.
  7. The project archive the report cites (SHA-256 93b7bd29…f38fb) is not among the files received.
