PR-GAP-2: What We Found
CAMS as a Leading Indicator of Societal Rupture
Kari Freyr McKern
Neural Nations Project · Sydney, Australia
neuralnations.org · GitHub: KaliBond/wintermute
Research Note  |  July 2026
Summary

The study tested one question: does CAMS — a framework that scores institutional coordination capacity across eight nodes — contain genuine predictive signal for societal ruptures, measurable before events occurred, rather than fitted after the fact? Across 44 societies, 4,541 society-years (1900–2025), and 126 coded rupture events, the answer is yes: a combined structural model reaches AUC 0.652 using only CAMS coordination metrics, no political, economic, or demographic covariates. The signal lives in slow-node degradation — institutional memory, governance capacity, inter-node coupling — not in fast-node operational noise. As of 2025, South Africa, Venezuela, and the USA are each at or above their own all-time CAMS stress readings.

The study tested one question: does CAMS contain genuine predictive signal for societal ruptures — not retrospective narrative-fitting, but actual forewarning measurable before events occurred?

The answer is yes.

The Panel

44 societies, 1900–2025, 4,541 society-years, 126 rupture events coded across 40 societies (coups, civil wars, revolutions, regime collapses, economic crises). A society-year was flagged "rupture-incoming" if an event occurred within the following three years — 334 pre-rupture years against 4,117 calm years, a 7.5% base rate.

What the Numbers Show

Every primary CAMS variable differs significantly between pre-rupture and calm years. Mean Stress in the three years before rupture averages 6.27 versus 5.36 in calm periods (p < 0.001). Helm Node Value (executive governance health) is 7.06 before ruptures versus 9.69 in calm periods. Bond Strength — how tightly the eight nodes are coupled — is 0.237 pre-rupture versus 0.297 in calm years. In AUC terms (0.5 = chance, 1.0 = perfect): Mean Stress scores 0.648, Bond Strength 0.641, S/K Ratio 0.637. A combined logistic model reaches AUC 0.652. For context, political science early-warning models using richer covariates — income, democracy scores, ethnicity, prior conflict history — typically land in the 0.68–0.75 range. Getting to 0.652 from structural coordination metrics alone is a meaningful result.

What Doesn't Predict Rupture

The Reactivity Ratio — fast operational nodes stressed relative to slow structural nodes — scores AUC 0.515, essentially chance. The rupture signal is not real-time operational chaos. It is slow structural degradation in the nodes that hold institutional memory, legal authority, and governance capacity. A society can be operationally chaotic for years without rupturing. What precedes rupture is the quiet erosion of the slow nodes.

The Mechanism, in One Line

Dominant logistic coefficients: Bond Strength (β = −0.54, low coupling → rupture) and Mean Stress (β = +0.38). Stress rising while nodes decouple from each other is the canonical pre-rupture signature.

The 2025 alarm Three societies are currently at or above their own all-time CAMS stress readings within the 125-year panel. South Africa is at 105% of its 1985 late-apartheid peak. Venezuela is at 104% of its 1989 petroleum-crisis reading. The United States is at 104% of its 1932 Great Depression reading — meaning 2025 US CAMS stress exceeds the worst year of the Depression in structural coordination terms. South Africa's Archive Node Value (institutional memory, legal fixation capacity) collapsed 10 points in five years (7.5 → −2.5), a degradation rate with no parallel in the panel outside active state-collapse events. The US Archive Node has dropped from 9.5 to 6.5 over the same period.

The One Thing the Model Can't Tell You

Whether current high-stress readings resolve through institutional reform or through rupture requires qualitative judgment about political agency that no metric system can provide. The model assigns elevated rupture probability; it cannot assign probability to the branch where the system pulls back.

This result sits alongside two earlier single-country studies on the United States — Study 1 on interest-burden absorption and Study 2 on legitimacy erosion — and points to the same underlying mechanism at both scales: CAMS slow-node degradation leads observable downstream outcomes (legitimacy erosion by 2–3 years, rupture incidence by 1–3 years), while fast-node metrics carry no signal.
