Every human organisation is a metasystem of adaptive humans. It cannot stop adapting, and it cannot adapt without strain. So it signals the stress of adapting all the time, whether anyone intends it or not, and writes that signal into the fabric of a planetary archive we now all share. The thesis, in one sentence

Why a book, on this site

Neural Nations holds datasets, dashboards, scoring tools and tests. It does not, by itself, tell you why anyone should have built them. This book does. It is my personal explanation for why the site exists and why the method works, written for anyone who would rather read a story than a specification.

It starts where Carl Sagan, Jacob Bronowski and David Grinspoon left off, and follows my own path from a playground I couldn't read, through a library, a long argument about wars and empires, and a collaboration with GPT and other frontier AI, to a method that other people and other machines can check. If the site is the instrument, the book is the account of why I built it and how to read it.

It is a personal explanation, not a peer-reviewed paper, and it says which statements are data, which are inference, and which are speculation. Every figure it draws from the CAMS record can be checked against this site and the open records behind it.

The idea, in four steps

  1. Organisations are made of adaptive people. A family, a firm and a nation are each a metasystem: they behave in ways none of their members does, and they outlast the members. You live inside several at once.
  2. Adapting costs, and it can't be switched off. Every adjustment to a changing world produces strain. We call it Stress. An organisation that stops adapting is only adapting badly.
  3. The strain is broadcast. Orders, budgets, complaints, verdicts, receipts and headlines are not reports of the organisation's condition. They are the organisation working, and its condition comes out in the working. Nobody has to intend the signal, which is why it is so hard to hide.
  4. The record is now common. Libraries, ledgers, newspapers, court files and web archives have been digitised and joined into one planetary archive that anyone can read. The method reads it the same way every time: eight functions, four readings, one number per function per year.

Everything else on this site follows from that. The eight functions are Helm, Shield, Lore, Stewards, Craft, Hands, Archive and Flow. The four readings are Coherence, Capacity, Stress and Abstraction. A group of any size, from a household to a state, can be read the same way.

Status of the claimThe one-sentence thesis above is the hypothesis the whole programme tests. It is not a result. The tests so far are on this site; the limits are set out once, plainly, in the book.

A score is a state, not a grade

The method makes no moral claim about which kind of society is better. Different histories produced different arrangements, each with its own strengths and its own typical failures. When a function fails, my first question has always been what was done to it, not who is to blame. The aim is forgiveness, not judgement, and a configuration that people can see well enough to change.

Read it, share it, correct it

The book is free, in both formats. It is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, the same open licence as the Zenodo deposits, so anyone may copy it, translate it and build on it as long as they say where it came from.

It was not written alone. Claude (Anthropic) drafted it with me from my words and records, checked the figures against the raw data and drew the charts. GPT (OpenAI) was my partner in building the model from 2024, and Grok (xAI), Kimi (Moonshot AI) and Gemini (Google) scored for it. Manus contributed passages to an earlier draft. The opinions, and the final say, are mine.
