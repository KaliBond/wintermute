What held Russia together as the old order broke apart? Which institutions could still get things done? Who could still move food, money and orders? Give two AIs the same prompt and see where their accounts part company. Then check the evidence and decide which one you’d back.

Preview the full prompt

The fortune gives you a reading of the year through eight jobs a society has to do. Helm steers. Shield defends. Lore makes meaning. Stewards hold resources. Craft makes things. Hands do the work. Archive remembers. Flow moves goods, money and messages.

Each paladin gets four vitals: Coherence, Capacity, Stress and Abstraction. Every score has to show its work. Look under the fortune for the evidence tier and dated fact behind each number. The page calculates each paladin’s Zeitgeist from the vitals. If the model can’t supply evidence, it has to say so. A missing vital leaves that Zeitgeist uncomputed. The session code ties the reply to your draw, and the cookie comes with a receipt.

02 · Crack it again

The second cookie is where it gets interesting

Same nation. Same year. Different AI.

Say both models give Russia’s Shield high Stress. Does one cite desertions while the other leans on battlefield losses? Those are different accounts of what was under pressure, even if the scores match. And if the scores diverge, you can go straight to the facts each model used and see which reading holds up.

Same nation. Different years.

Try Russia in 1913, 1917 and 1921. Follow Britain through two world wars, or China from the last emperor to the Olympic year. Collect the cookies and a ribbon begins to form. Watch which functions come under strain first, which keep working through the shock and which take longest to recover.

03 · What the lens looks at

Look past the balcony

History gives us plenty of kings, presidents and generals. CAMS asks what they could actually get done. Could the centre get orders carried out? Could the roads and railways keep food moving? Could officials find the records they needed? A leader can sign a decree in an afternoon. Getting a country to act on it is a different proposition.

The same questions apply in Washington, London, Beijing and Moscow. The scores describe how the society was organised. High Stress means pressure, and a function can keep working under a great deal of it.

Each cookie is one model’s reading on one run. Two models can agree and still lean on the same weak evidence. That’s a reason to open the ledger.
