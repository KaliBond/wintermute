A Neural Nations CAMS Series

The Borrowed Nation

Five essays on Australia's 175-year architecture of strategic dependency.

By Kari Freyr McKern · August 2026 · ORCID 0009-0007-9045-0505

Robert Menzies called it needing "great and powerful friends." The phrase has outlasted the British Empire, survived the Cold War, and now frames the case for a nuclear submarine program costing hundreds of billions of dollars. This series uses the CAMS-CAN framework to argue that the doctrine is not a foreign policy choice but the reproduction of a structural condition built into Australia's institutions at colonial founding: a developed Helm and Stewards without a developed Shield.

Across five essays, the series traces that structural gap from the 1850 colonial baseline through Federation, the fall of Singapore, and the transfer of patron from London to Washington; examines why Australia's capital-owning Stewards node has never been domestically coherent; measures — across 105 years of Trove and Hansard data — why parliamentary China-threat discourse tracks Australian domestic stress rather than Chinese behaviour; documents the Flow Paradox in which Australia's largest trading partner is also its named strategic threat; and closes with a disciplined counterfactual history of the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership signed in 2014, and the different institutional path that was available but not taken.

Essay One
The Inheritance of Dependence

A developed Helm and Stewards without a developed Shield — the colonial-era condition from which "great and powerful friends" follows.
Essay Two
The Colonised Stewards

From the squattocracy to Rio Tinto and BHP: why Australia's capital-owning class has never been domestically coherent, and why only Chinese investment triggers regulatory alarm.
Essay Three
The Anxiety Machine

105 years of Trove and Hansard data: parliamentary China-threat discourse tracks domestic stress, not Chinese behaviour — and since 2006 the correlation with China's actual institutional health has flipped negative.
Essay Four
The Flow Paradox

Australia sells to the world it claims to fear. China takes 32–38% of exports and 63% of maritime tonnage; the United States takes under 5%.
Essay Five
The Road Not Taken

A disciplined counterfactual history of the 2014 Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, and the CAMS node architecture Australia would show today had it been built out instead of dismantled.
Kari Freyr McKern is an independent researcher and systems analyst based in Sydney, Australia, and creator of the CAMS-CAN framework.
