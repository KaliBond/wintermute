Tier 1: works immediately, explains itself

Start here. No prerequisites beyond curiosity; each tool says what it does on the page and pays off inside a minute.

1

JUNO Formulation: CAMS Calculator

Runs in browser For: newcomers · Effort: type 32 integers, click Compute

Type raw C/K/S/A scores for the eight nodes; get Node Values, bond strengths, S/K ratio, System Bond Λ, Praetorian Index, attractor basin and the Robson Gauge, plus a copyable CSV. Every formula is printed on the page.

Why first: zero dependencies, zero jargon-gating, and the formulas sit next to the outputs, the only tool a sceptic can fully audit in one sitting.

2

CAMS-CAN DIY Kit: Practitioner & Skeptic Field Guide

Download For: newcomers and sceptics · Effort: 4 files, ~4 KB, no key, no install

Everything to run CAMS-CAN scoring by hand inside a Claude Project (project instructions, two blank schema templates, README) alongside the reliability evidence for deciding whether to believe the output. Instructions and templates are reproduced in full on the page.

Why second: the lowest-friction on-ramp to actually running the method yourself, and the trust evidence travels with the tool rather than being buried elsewhere.

3

Story Maker (and the Aha! variant)

Runs in browser For: curious readers · Effort: pick dataset, pick masking profile, download

Turns a CAMS era-means dataset into a name-masked story packet (data, a ready-to-use AI prompt, reading rules, README) so a fresh AI session can recover the story blind. States plainly that masking is not anonymity.

Why third: a two-step flow with exactly one decision at each step and an honest caveat where it belongs. A model for how the denser tools should introduce themselves.

4

CAMS Scorer: Live

Runs in browser For: curious readers · Effort: name an entity, set a year range, wait for three passes

Scores any country, company or city on demand: three isolated Kimi passes read the historical record and are ensembled into node-level C/K/S/A with an uncertainty envelope.

Why here: the clearest value proposition on the site (“score anything”), but it runs on a live backend, so latency and availability sit outside the reader’s control, and the page could say more about what the envelope means before you click.

5

CAMS Scorer: Local Edition

DownloadNeeds API key For: readers who want control · Effort: unzip ~22 MB, double-click, paste your own key

The same scoring ensemble running entirely on your machine with your own OpenAI, Grok, Claude or Kimi account: nothing sent to Neural Nations, billed by your provider. GUI and command-line builds; runs capped at 9 years, 1/3/5 passes.

Why here: excellent documentation and honest cost disclosure, but it asks for a download plus a paid key, two friction steps the browser tools don’t have.

Tier 2: rewarding, but assumes CAMS literacy

Well built and honest; you will get more out of them after reading What Is CAMS? or spending five minutes in Tier 1.

6

CAMS Compare

Runs in browser For: CAMS-literate readers · Effort: pick two societies, scrub the year slider

Two societies, one kernel: V̄, κ, λ₂, phase space and structural events computed live from the 8×4 ensemble matrix. Every panel carries a one-line gloss (e.g. “falling λ₂ = fragmenting network”).

Why sixth: the best-labelled of the dense dashboards (the panels explain their own symbols) but “structural events” and attractor basins only mean something if you already know the model.

7

CAMS Dashboard: USA, Iran, Saudi Arabia, Israel (2015–2026)

Runs in browser For: newcomers · Effort: read four annotated charts

Quarterly scoring of four societies through shocks and turning points, with a plain-English “What is this?” box, per-domain breakdowns, stress heatmaps and a key-events list telling you where to look.

Why seventh: the most accessible of the data dashboards precisely because it narrows the scope to four societies and annotates the history, a template for onboarding the bigger explorers.

8

CAMS Explorer: Thermodynamics of History

Runs in browser For: CAMS-literate readers · Effort: pick a society, step through history

Select any of 45 societies and step year by year while node values, bond strengths and Λ(t) update in real time, with a failure-modes hierarchy alongside.

Why eighth: powerful time-travel mechanic, thin onboarding; the page assumes you know what Λ(t) is and why the hierarchy matters.

9

Zeitgeist Detector

Runs in browser For: curious readers · Effort: pick society, press play

Tracks A*·C·(K–S) (symbolic richness × coherence × surplus) as a “civilisational vitality” signal across 45 societies, with a rank ladder and playback.

Why ninth: playful and immediate, but the composite is the least self-evident quantity on the site; one worked example of the arithmetic would lift it a tier.

10

CAMS Mind Reader

Runs in browser For: CAMS-literate readers · Effort: run the pipeline, read the classification

Computes each node’s cognitive activation, reconstructs the JUNO bond matrix, measures global synchronisability via the spectral gap and classifies the system’s attractor state: societies as distributed cognitive organisms.

Why tenth: the pipeline narrative is clearly written; the outputs (λ₂, activation vectors) land somewhere between tool and research output, so it rewards readers who like the maths.

11

JUNO Dataset Explorer

Runs in browser For: specialists · Effort: choose from 47,960 node-years

The full JUNO unified dataset (53 societies, 8 nodes, 1750–2026) as trajectory charts, thermodynamic phase portraits and per-node stress-level and coupling diagnostics.

Why eleventh: the most complete data window on the site and honest about it, but the sheer surface area needs a guide; the stats banner up top tells you how much there is, not where to start.

12

CAMS Instrument

Runs in browser For: CAMS-literate readers · Effort: one society, many panels, built-in field guide

One society read as a living system: eight-node network, phase-space trail, four modes, and the Ψ epiphenomena field, with a “reading the instrument” guide actually built into the page.

Why twelfth: the built-in field guide is exactly the right pattern; the panel density still makes the first five minutes steep.

13

Morphospace Atlas

Runs in browser For: specialists · Effort: a long, careful read

A formal typology of eight civilisational attractor types with threshold rules, node-activation profiles and coupling signatures: designed so a blinded case can be scored and classified without its name. Explicitly provisional (“the minimum set recovered so far”).

Why thirteenth: superb scientific writing and honest scope; it ranks lower on usability purely because it is a document you study, not a tool you operate.

14

CAMS Network Explorer: Institutional Weather

Runs in browser For: CAMS-literate readers · Effort: pick a scenario, or nudge a node yourself

An 8-node coordination network you can perturb: pick a scenario, scrub time, or nudge one node and watch the system respond. Carries the site’s best caveat practice: “bounded exploratory dynamics, not calibrated prediction”, at the top.

Why fourteenth: the interactive what-if is engaging and the limits are stated up front; the controls assume you know which node is which and why you’d nudge it.

15

Seshat × CAMS: Cross-Dataset Validation

Runs in browser For: specialists · Effort: read one careful comparison

Compares Seshat Social Complexity PC1 against CAMS Λ(t) for Latium Vetus, CE 460–1800: N=14 century-level observations, Pearson r = −0.31, Spearman ρ = 0.00. A negative result, published anyway.

Why fifteenth: small, focused and honest; scientifically valuable, but a null on N=14 is a specialist interest rather than a general instrument.

Tier 3: powerful, dense, or partially superseded

Worth the climb for researchers already inside the framework. Several overlap; consolidation would help more than polish.

16

Escalation Archetypes

Runs in browser For: CAMS-literate readers · Effort: work through five causal-loop diagrams

The five archetypal pathways from institutional stress to systemic crisis, drawn as causal loop diagrams.

Why here: CLDs are the least self-explanatory visual idiom on the site; no loop is obvious to a first reader without a walk-through. Pairs well with the Failure Modes Story below.

17

Failure Modes Map

Runs in browser For: CAMS-literate readers · Effort: navigate one dense CLD

Interactive causal loop diagram of the four failure spirals: R1 Flow–Hands death spiral, R2 rent-seeking trap, R3 Shield capture, R4 institutional amnesia.

Why seventeenth: the content is the framework’s most legible applied contribution; the map format keeps it from newcomers. The companion story (next entry) is the better first contact.

18

Failure Modes Story

Read / narrative For: newcomers · Effort: read one essay

The narrative companion to the Failure Modes Map: how complex systems drift into collapse, from coordinated decline to institutional capture, in plain prose.

Why eighteenth (and should be promoted): as a tool it’s just prose, but it is the most humane entry point to the framework’s darkest material; link it before the map, not after.

19

CAMS Advanced Analysis

Runs in browser For: specialists · Effort: bring your own questions

Deep-dive visualisations: node trajectories, ESCH stress profiles, cross-layer bond networks and Λ(t) regime detection across 38 societies.

Why nineteenth: depth without a map, powerful if you arrive with a question, bewildering if you don’t.

20

CAMS-CAN Integrated Dashboard

Runs in browser For: specialists · Effort: untangle two frameworks at once

Combined CAMS and CAN dashboard for integrated reading of coordination efficiency and stress levels.

Why twentieth: the least-introduced of the dashboards; it presumes fluency in both formalisms it merges. A candidate for merging with Advanced Analysis or the Explorer.

21

Granger Causality Validator

Runs in browser For: specialists · Effort: set lag, noise, series length

In-browser synthetic validation suite: a three-scenario stress test with adjustable lag, noise and series length, probing Granger causality in CAMS-structured time series.

Why twenty-first: a methods tool for methods readers, genuinely useful, but its whole purpose is auditing one statistical claim.

22

Civilisational Attractor Space + 3D Attractor

Runs in browser For: specialists · Effort: learn to fly a 3D phase space

Two overlapping 3D renderings of civilisational attractors (Plotly): trajectories as far-from-equilibrium metabolic networks; C, S and A as state axes. Isolate, compare, rotate.

Why twenty-second: the prettiest objects on the site and the hardest to read without a guide, and the two pages duplicate each other. Consolidate, then explain the axes with one worked society.

23

Mindscapes

Read / narrative For: curious readers · Effort: read a mood report

Claude-generated Vision–Affect and Mythic Attractor reports on national mood: France and Norway live, pipeline open.

Why twenty-third: engaging writing, but only two countries and the interpretive layer (“mood”) is the framework’s least-defended move; readers should meet it after the structural tools.

24

How Societies Coordinate

Read / narrative For: newcomers · Effort: read the theory once

The coordination theory underneath the instruments: eight functional nodes, four metrics, and the bond network that determines resilience to stress.

Why twenty-fourth: filed under Tools but it is an explainer; in this layout it belongs to Orientation on the Everything Else page, one click from the tools that need it.

Usability verdict: the strongest pattern on the site is honesty, limits stated at the top, failures published. The weakest is onboarding: a dozen dashboards open cold, assuming the reader already speaks CAMS. Moving JUNO Calculator, the DIY Kit and Story Maker to the front of the Tools menu, and giving each Tier 2/3 dashboard a three-line “start here” box modelled on the 4-Society Dashboard’s, would lift the whole tier without touching a line of their internals.

Added 20 November 2026

Tools published after Kimi’s ranking was written. Listed here unranked, in no order of merit; descriptions are drawn from each tool’s own page or its Explore listing.

  • CAMS History: Atlas, Blind Prophet and Primer: read the JUNO panel on the CAMS instruments, then call the crisis before the year arrives.
  • CAMS Global Stress Dashboard: Observed-only readout of the eight-node by four-metric CAMS matrix: active pathology signatures, the Λ coordination index and historical analogue matching. No projection.
  • Deliberative Decay & Lore Misdirection (DDI × LMI): A diagnostic matrix setting institutional atrophy against ideological capture, with a trajectory view and a snapshot slider.
  • Social Cognition in Real Time: Collective mood signals read as an epiphenomenon of meaning and metabolism.

Note added 20 November 2026: the “start here” boxes recommended in the usability verdict above are now on six dashboards (Explorer, JUNO Dataset Explorer, Compare, Instrument, Advanced Analysis, Integrated Dashboard).

Added 24 November 2026

Listed unranked. The laboratory is a constructed toy; findings on that page are conditional.

  • CAMS Shannon Laboratory: Interactive estimator bench (v2.2) for agreement, learning and information flow. Muted-source baselines, environment-conditioned transfer entropy, the authority echo, and the memory-worth test. All computation runs in the browser.
  • CAMS from an Informational Perspective: Research note on the Shannon layer after an independent audit, with pre-registration candidates INFO-1 to INFO-8. All simulation findings are conditional on the toy.
