The Meta-Animal Under the Microscope

No society is entitled to explain itself before it has been compared with history. That includes our own; it includes CAMS.

Kari McKern · ComplexityWorkz · September 2026

Every great power tells a story in which its conduct is exceptional and its necessities are universal. The United States speaks of a rules-based order, though the rules bend around its wars. China speaks of rejuvenation and non-interference, Russia of encirclement, Europe of values, while the supply chains beneath rarely appear in the moral portrait. Every society remembers what vindicates it and forgets what embarrasses it. This is not proof that all claims are false. It is proof that narrative cannot be its own court of appeal.

Nor is the answer to swap one civilisational story for its rival; the dissident who merely reverses the signs leaves the machinery of power hidden beneath new costumes.

An instrument that can embarrass its operator

We need an instrument that asks the same questions of friends and adversaries and can return an answer inconvenient to its operator. CAMS, the Complex Adaptive Model of Societies, is my attempt to build it. Its governing rule is simple: no society is entitled to explain itself before it has been compared with history. That includes our own; it includes CAMS.

The meta-animal, without Spencer's ladder

The starting claim is that a human society belongs to the broad family of bonded, surplus-dependent adaptive systems. A nation is not literally an enlarged chimpanzee troop, yet beneath the institutional differences lies an older problem. Any social unit must coordinate action, manage conflict, distribute resources, preserve knowledge and respond to threat. The comparison does not reduce civilisation to zoology; it identifies the functional domain from which civilisation emerged. The ancestry is compromised: Herbert Spencer's social organism arranged peoples on a ladder and travelled easily into eugenics. CAMS parts company with Spencer at the root. Its meta-animal is functional, not racial; diagnostic, not normative. A score is a state, not a grade. A trajectory is a history, not a destiny.

The sybond

I call the social bond at national scale the sybond: the web of recognitions, obligations, symbols and records through which people who will never meet act as members of one system. A currency, a census, a court, a flag; these are not ornaments but working prostheses. The distinctively human development is the cumulative externalisation of memory and technique, Tomasello's ratchet: an exoskeleton of law, record, model and method that is our great adaptive advantage, and also the place where our abstractions learn to mistake themselves for reality.

The cost of bonding

Bonding is costly, so the sybond must create or preserve enough usable margin to pay for the coordination that sustains it. The margin is biophysical, coordinative and distributive, and the allocation of gains among members, classes, regions and generations is not an afterthought. Where membership does not outperform accessible alternatives, persistence depends on blocked exit, legitimating narrative and coercion, and coercion consumes what it protects. Soil, trust and institutional competence may be consumed as if they were income; for a time, drawdown resembles prosperity.

Eight functions, four vitals

CAMS proposes that every durable society performs eight recurrent functions: Helm for strategic direction, Shield for security, Lore for shared meaning, Stewards for administration, Craft for technique, Hands for labour, Archive for institutional memory, and Flow for the circulation of goods, information, trust and dissent, including the exits through which pressure escapes. Each is assessed across four vitals: Coherence, Capacity, Stress, and the deliberately double-edged Abstraction, which can preserve principle beyond the ruler or detach into maps multiplying while the territory deteriorates. The taxonomy is a wager under test; if six nodes or ten do better, CAMS must change.

Four lenses

The method applies four lenses. The functional lens reads beneath political labels and relocates ideology from judge to evidence. The temporal lens places the present claim inside a long sequence, catching stock liquidation dressed as growth. The comparative lens applies one scale to Rome, Song China, the Soviet Union and the United States alike. The relational lens examines couplings, since a coherent Helm may coordinate the Stewards or capture the Archive. The emerging Relational Grammar Postulate holds that local functional relations are conserved while each society assembles them into a distinctive historical morphology, a path-dependent national fingerprint, not an essence.

Honest limits

The instrument carries honest limits. Its language-model scorers share training materials and historiographic priors; ensemble agreement demonstrates reproducibility, not independence. A Stress-Flow prediction drawn from animal behaviour failed its first panel test; a narrower interaction version survives, pending stricter controls. The framework learned something at the expense of its author, and I count that as the best result in the file. CAMS cannot yet claim a uniquely correct ontology, causal mechanisms, or the prediction of particular ruptures. A framework that cannot specify what would defeat it has joined the narratives it was built to examine.

No exemptions

Contemporary societies have become dangerously proficient at describing themselves while losing the ability to inspect themselves. Abstraction without historical comparison is an echo chamber with equations. CAMS interrupts that loop: it begins beneath ideology and restores time, comparison, relationship and the possibility of refusal. Historical comparison does not produce moral equivalence. It prevents moral immunity. That is the wager. No exemptions. Not Washington. Not Beijing. Not Moscow. Not us.

Peace, people.
