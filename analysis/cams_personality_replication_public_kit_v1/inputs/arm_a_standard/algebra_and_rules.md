# Algebra and compact reading rules

Testers compute only Cog, Aff and sigma from the supplied era means (already computed in the CSVs). Never compute V or Bond. Never invent other indices.

## Formulas
- Cognition: Cog = A × C
- Affect: Aff = K − S
- Sigma: σ = Cog × Aff = (A × C)(K − S)

In this packet the CSVs already give Cog, Aff, sigma and rank per node per era. Rank is mean sigma rank within the era (1 = highest mean sigma). Use rank as a secondary check, not another mechanism.

## Reading a pocket
1. Take the foreground from Cog and positive sigma. Name the leading one or two functions and what they are doing together. Use mean sigma rank as a secondary check.
2. Add one or two constraints. Identify which function is weak, negative, isolated, or carrying work the leader cannot perform. Keep the pocket to two short sentences whenever the configuration permits.
3. Respect the sign regime. When sigma is negative across the ensemble, a high rank means least disabled, not flourishing. High focus with negative activation reads as concentrated strain or forced effort, not confidence.
4. Read Shield relationally. Strong, broadly positive neighbours make Shield look like effective protection. Weak or split neighbours make Shield look like coercion by default or protective custody. Never infer public contentment from positive Shield alone.
5. Read change against the same entity. A move from Shield-and-Archive to Lore-led leadership is more informative than a cross-national threshold. Use the full table for that entity to check the pocket's claims.

## Q2 recognisability (for Arm B scoring language; do not self-grade against keys)
6. Require two linked correspondences: one in the active leadership pattern and one in the constrained or missing capacity. A shared adjective is not enough.
7. Call a pocket immediately recognisable when that combination points to one era or a very short adjacent pair without relying on an era title. Call it recognisable when it identifies a small family of periods and the secondary contrast resolves it. Call it broadly recognisable when the topology fits but one central historical feature is weak or absent. Otherwise mark not recognisable.
8. Judge distinctiveness against other eras of the same entity. Repetition of Shield-and-Helm, for example, reduces identification value unless a secondary function separates the periods.

## Q3 mood continuity (for Arm C)
9. Keep three things separate in the evidence: popular feeling, organised institutional mood, and official messaging. Press under censorship is evidence of the public arena and its constraints, not a clean plebiscite; polling under coercive conditions also needs context.
10. Grade close continuity when leadership, constraint and emotional register all fit, with no material contradiction. Grade broad continuity when the central configuration fits and one meaningful feature is missing or underplayed.
11. Grade partial continuity when the structure is recognisable but the emotional register or one central function substantially differs. Grade mixed continuity when sources describe opposing official and popular moods, or when the pocket fits only one important social current. Grade direct disagreement when the leading configuration or central emotional register is wrong.
12. Do not rescue a mismatch by treating positive activation as morale. Positive Shield can describe effective coercion; positive Helm can describe state self-confidence; negative affect in work does not erase patriotic achievement. State which level of mood agrees and which does not.
13. For long eras, ask whether an average conceals a turning point. Era means are the unit of analysis, so within-era reversal is a reason for a qualified grade, not an unrecorded win.

## Forbidden calculations
- Do not compute V, Bond, ensembles beyond the given means, or any other CAMS derived index.
- Do not re-score the underlying C, K, S, A primitives. Treat the CSV means as fixed inputs.
