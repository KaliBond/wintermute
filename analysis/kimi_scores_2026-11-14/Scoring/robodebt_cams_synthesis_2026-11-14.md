# What the CAMS Register Actually Establishes About Robodebt

*A synthesis for the essay file — Kari McKern, 2026-11-14*

The argument about robodebt is usually conducted at two unsatisfying extremes: it was the worst thing an Australian government ever did to its own citizens, or it was a well-meaning compliance failure with bad optics. A pre-analysis register of seventy-four committed questions, run against scored panels and replicated by independent scorers, supports neither. It supports three findings, robust across every check applied to them, and a residue of things the evidence cannot reach. The findings are more interesting than the polemics, and the residue is where intellectual honesty lives.

**The agency signature.** The register committed, before any agency-level data existed, to three tests: executive change must precede agency deterioration; policy must persist after dated warnings; and the deterioration must be concentrated in the robodebt-relevant institution rather than general across the state. All three passed, and all three survived independent replication. Centrelink's recipient-facing capacity began falling in the same quarter the 2015 budget's compliance measure commenced, fell two points below its baseline, held the floor for four years through the Ombudsman report, the Senate report and the tribunal judgments, and never recovered until after the class action settled. The ATO — same executive, same data-matching environment, and in 2020 the same pandemic shock — never left its baseline, and delivered JobKeeper at its highest capacity while Centrelink buckled. The state did not fail everywhere. It failed *there*, *then*, and against *those warnings*. That specificity is the strongest thing evidence can say about executive responsibility without pretending to read minds.

**Selective capacity.** The same measurement rule applied to two programmes of the same government: thirty-one months from the Ombudsman's warning to the cessation of unlawful debt raising; about five months from the vaccine rollout's visible failure to its correction. A six-to-fifty-fold difference in correction latency, between a programme aimed at welfare recipients and one aimed at businesses and the vaccinated public. The state that could not stop averaging incomes for four years could re-plan a national immunisation campaign in a season. Whatever explains the difference — and the data do not say — it is not technical complexity and it is not general incapacity. It is constituency-conditional correction: the feedback loop from evidence to remedy runs at a speed set by who is on the receiving end of the harm.

**Mechanism-only exceptionality.** Against a locked comparison set of seven Australian governments from 2007 to 2023, with criteria frozen in advance and applied identically to both parties, the "no precedent" claim fails. Australia had prior state harms at comparable or greater scale, with official unlawfulness findings (the PNG Supreme Court's 2016 ruling on Manus; the $70 million false-imprisonment settlement), with class-based targeting (compulsory income management of roughly 20,000 people, overwhelmingly Indigenous, under the suspended Racial Discrimination Act), and with correction delays longer than robodebt's thirty-one months (offshore detention ran against continuous warning for years and was not remedied within the window). Some of those precedents belong to Labor governments, some to Coalition ones; the screen does not care, and neither should we. What robodebt genuinely pioneered is the mechanism: the industrialisation of administrative unlawfulness — legal fiction converted into hundreds of thousands of machine-generated debts. Its exceptionality is real, and it is narrower than its rhetoric.

**The residue.** Three things the register deliberately cannot establish. *Intent*: no score speaks to motive, and the external record — the Royal Commission's findings, NACC Operation Myrtleford's corrupt-conduct findings against two officials and non-findings against the referred ministers — supports deterrence-of-criticism as media strategy, but not the demoralisation or schadenfreude hypotheses at the level of personal purpose. The scheme was a demoralising blow in effect; whether it was one in intent remains unproven, and the distinction matters. *Origination*: the bureaucratic-misinformation account and the executive-direction account are both partially supported by the external record; the scores cannot arbitrate between them. *Person-level attribution*: no CAMS cell carries validated information about any individual.

**The methodological moral.** The register's discipline — frozen tests, not-testable classifications, ensemble replication, adverse results reported prominently — did not slow the analysis down. It made the analysis sayable. Every finding above survived because someone tried to kill it first. The one specification defect the freeze exposed (a scale threshold calibrated to robodebt's own numbers) is reported in the open precisely because it could not be quietly adjusted. A claim that survives its own attempted falsification is worth more than a stronger claim that was never at risk. The state's failure, in the end, is nameable without hyperbole: not proven cruelty, but a proven, specific, constituency-conditional failure of corrective feedback — visible in the record three years before the scheme stopped, and corrected only when the constituency least able to complain was joined in the evidence by everyone else.
