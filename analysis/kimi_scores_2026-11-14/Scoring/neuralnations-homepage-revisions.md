## 1. Revised opening (replaces the current first two paragraphs)

> This is not another theory about why societies rise and fall — it is a
> measurement system.
>
> The premise is simple: human societies are complex adaptive systems, and
> their dynamics leave traces in the historical record. CAMS recovers those
> traces and makes them measurable through eight institutional nodes —
> security, knowledge systems, material conditions, strategic direction,
> memory, execution, resource stewardship, and circulation (Shield, Lore,
> Craft, Helm, Archive, Hands, Stewards, Flow).
>
> CAMS tracks how these nodes interact, and how those interactions evolve over
> time. When the nodes are well-coupled, societies tend to be resilient. When
> they decouple, stress tends to accumulate faster than adaptive capacity, and
> coordination fails. These dynamics are visible in the data years — sometimes
> decades — before they become visible in events.

Notes:

- The tagline moves from the bottom of the page to the top; the old abstract
  opener ("The foundational insight is…") is deleted.
- Node names added in parentheses to bridge to the Framework vocabulary.
- Both resilience and failure are now stated as tendencies, matching the
  epistemic register of the method note.
- "Legible… obvious" tautology replaced with "visible in the data… visible in
  events".
