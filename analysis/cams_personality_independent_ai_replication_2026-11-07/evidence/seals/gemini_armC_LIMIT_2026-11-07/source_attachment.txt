 * **Structural & Mood Strengths:** Russia's dataset shows strong sign-regime contrast across its 23 eras. All four all-negative regimes (VI 1905–1906, VIII 1915–1917, IX 1918–1921, and XIX 1992–1999) separated cleanly in both Arm A and Arm B because the algebra distinguishes low-cognition "least disabled" survival channels (Stewards, Flow, Craft) from high-cognition negative-strain command (Helm, Shield in IX War Communism) or collapsed command (XIX 1990s). Similarly, single-era topological shifts—such as Craft and Lore surging alongside Shield in XIV (1954–1964 Thaw), Lore jumping to rank 1 while Helm and Archive turn negative in XVII–XVIII (1986–1991 Glasnost and dissolution), and Archive standing alone in positive activation in XXIII (2022–2025)—mapped closely to historical scholarship.
 * **Positive-Affect Ceiling Nuance:** Era XXI (2009–2013) was graded broad rather than close (popular: partial) because the all-positive ensemble slightly smooths the 2011–2013 urban Bolotnaya protest wave, even though the relative rank order (Lore at rank 6.4 and Hands at rank 7.0 behind dominant Shield and Helm) still captured the structural constraint.
#### 2. Australia (CORE — 18 Eras)
 * **Structural & Mood Strengths:** Australia's eras are anchored by recurring Archive, Flow, and Lore leadership, punctuated by sharp negative sign shifts on Helm and Hands during crises (III 1915–1918 conscription split and 1917 strike; V 1929–1939 Depression; X 1973–1975 stagflation and constitutional dismissal; XV–XVI 2008–2019 leadership spills and wage stagnation; XVII 2020–2021 border closures and lockdowns). Particularly diagnostic was Era XIV (1996–2007), where Shield was the sole negative node (-18.75) inside an otherwise booming Flow-led register—matching the Tampa/Pacific Solution border controversies and Iraq War security divisions.
 * **Long-Window Smoothing:** Eras VII (1946–1949) and VIII (1950–1965) were graded broad because high positive registers slightly flatten late-1940s rationing/coal-strike friction and early-1950s Cold War/1961 credit-squeeze episodes.
#### 3. United States (CORE — 15 Eras)
 * **Structural & Mood Strengths:** The compact relational reading rule for Shield proved essential in USA XIV (1929–1932), where Shield (+29.04, rank 1.25) led only because Flow, Hands, Stewards, Helm, and Craft had collapsed into deep negative activation—matching defensive policing and the Bonus Army dispersal rather than flourishing morale. Equally distinct was the persistent isolation of Hands at rank 7.8–8.0 during high-growth eras (XI Progressive Era, XIII 1920s, XX 1980s, XXI 1990s), capturing lagging labour share beneath booming Flow, Archive, and Helm. In recent eras (XXIV 2016–2019 and XXV 2020–2025), the sharp divergence between positive Craft/Archive/Shield and deeply negative Helm/Lore aligned closely with institutional continuity operating amid acute executive and narrative polarisation.
 * **Long-Window Smoothing:** Era XVII (1946–1962) was graded broad because the 17-year all-positive window averages across the 1946 strike wave and McCarthy-era tensions.
#### 4. Denmark (LIMIT Negative Control — 19 Graded Eras)
 * **Where Denmark Succeeded:** When the underlying coding had period-specific observations, the algebra worked well:
   * **Era XIII (1940–1945 Occupation):** Lore, Hands, and high-focus Archive led while Shield dropped into isolated negative activation (-13.33, rank 8)—matching the Alsang national song movement, permanent-secretary administrative continuity (*Departementschefstyret*), and the disarmament of the Danish military and police (close).
   * **Era I (1752–1783):** Stewards, Helm, and Craft led while Archive was negative (-30), capturing enlightened absolutism and the Struensee cabinet-order administrative upheaval (broad).
   * **Era XVII (1990–2001):** Craft and Archive led with Hands, Flow, and Stewards strong while Shield and Euro-sceptic Lore (1992 Maastricht and 2000 euro referendum rejections) trailed at the bottom (close).
 * **Where Denmark Broke or Hit the Sparse-Input Limiter:** Denmark's CSV contains four multi-era blocks with **byte-for-byte identical numerical vectors** carried forward across adjacent periods (II=III, V=VI=VII=VIII, X=XI=XII, and XVIII=XIX=XX), plus two post-war eras (XIV and XV) with **exact six-node ties**, and only two negative node values across 273 years (Archive in I, Shield in XIII):
   * **Direct Disagreement (Era III, 1807–1813):** Carrying forward the prosperous 1784–1806 vector unchanged into 1807–1813 produced an all-positive Craft-and-Stewards pocket during the British Bombardment of Copenhagen, the loss of the fleet, the Gunboat War blockade, and the 1813 State Bankruptcy (*Statsbankerotten*).
   * **Mixed Continuity (Era VII, 1864–1873):** Carrying forward the 1840–1863 vector unchanged into 1864–1873 missed the catastrophic 1864 Second Schleswig War defeat (official: disagree), even though its Craft, Hands, and Stewards foreground fit the subsequent Dalgas heath-reclamation movement.
   * **Partial Continuity (Eras IV, V, VIII, XI, XVI, XX):** All-positive carry-forward vectors flattened major episodic crises, including the 1820s agricultural depression (IV), the 1848–1850 First Schleswig War (V), Estrup's 1877–1894 provisional-laws constitutional crisis (VIII), the 1920 Easter Crisis and 1931–1932 unemployment spike (XI), 1970s–1980s stagflation/austerity (XVI), and the 2020 mink-cull scandal plus post-2022 defence surge (XX).
### 3.4 Differences Among Popular, Institutional, and Official Mood
Separating popular, institutional, and official mood in Arm C prevented false positives and clarified what the eight nodes actually register:
 1. **High Helm + Shield with Negative Hands + Lore (Authoritarian / Coercive Consolidation):** In Imperial and Stalinist Russia (I, II, IV, XI, XIII), positive Helm and Shield reflected official state self-confidence and institutional security enforcement, while negative Hands and Lore registered popular exhaustion and censored public speech. Because the Arm A pockets explicitly named both the coercive foreground and the depleted Hands/Lore constraints, all three mood levels agreed with the composite portrait without mistaking positive Shield for popular contentment.
 2. **High Archive + Flow with Negative Helm + Lore (Institutional Continuity Under Political Polarisation):** In recent Australian (XV, XVI) and US (XIX, XXIV, XXV) eras, institutional and commercial machinery (Archive, Flow, Craft) remained positive while official executive authority (Helm) and shared public narrative (Lore) turned negative. Separating institutional mood from official and popular mood confirmed that bureaucratic and market systems can operate effectively during periods of acute political frustration.
 3. **All-Positive Registers Flattening Popular or Official Shocks:** Where divergences appeared between mood levels (such as Russia XXI popular: partial, Denmark VII official: disagree, and Denmark IV, VIII, XI, XVI popular: partial), they occurred almost exclusively in all-positive ensembles where a multi-year mean or sparse carry-forward vector smoothed over a sharp popular protest or military/constitutional shock.
### 3.5 Omissions, Source Limitations, and Methodological Conclusions
 1. **Disclosed Omissions:**
   * **Denmark IX (1901–1913):** Unobserved in era_means_denmark.csv; omitted from Arm A and Arm C per protocol.
   * **EXTENSION (China, UK, Norway — 51 eras):** Held during Arm A per Turn 2 instructions and therefore omitted and disclosed in Arm C (armC_EXTENSION.jsonl) rather than generating post-seal pockets after Arm B and web activation.
 2. **Source Limitations:** For 19th-century Russia (I–V) and 18th-century Denmark (I–II), contemporary popular mood is not measured by polling and must be inferred through institutional records (peasant disturbances, strike counts, petition drives, and censorship archives) documented in retrospective reference histories (*Britannica*, national archives).
 3. **Overall Methodological Verdict:** Across the 56 CORE eras, the lean algebra (\text{Cog} = A \times C, \text{Aff} = K - S, \sigma = \text{Cog} \times \text{Aff}) and two-sentence functional pocket rules achieved 100% blind topological identification in Arm B (54 immediate, 2 recognisable) and 100% close-or-broad historical mood continuity in Arm C (52 close, 4 broad). Meanwhile, Denmark functioned exactly as intended as a negative control: where annual observations were sparse and carried forward across multi-era blocks or tied across six nodes, both Arm B era discrimination and Arm C mood continuity degraded visibly and predictably (1 direct_disagreement, 1 mixed, 5 partial). The method succeeds when fed genuine era-level variance and fails transparently when fed flat carry-forward means.
