3. FINDINGS BY ENTITY AND SET
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

CORE — Russia (23 eras, 2 broad)
  Russia was the most grade-stable entity in CORE. 21 of 23 eras returned close.
  The two broad cases were Era III (1801–1824: Helm-led topology during Alexander I's
  oscillation between liberalism and reaction, where official confidence exceeded
  popular mood) and Era XXIII (2014–2026: Lore-negative topology correctly captures
  institutional narrative degradation but misses the intensity of state-propaganda
  activation, which generates high positive official-Lore energy even as the
  structural Lore node decays). The Lore-node tension — negative structural topology
  vs high official-propaganda output — is Russia's distinctive Arm C challenge and
  produced the XXIII broad grade.

CORE — Australia (18 eras, 6 broad)
  Australia had the highest broad rate in CORE (6/18 = 33%). Common factor: the
  CAMS topology underweights the colonial-racial structure of Australian society
  before 1970. Eras IV, XI, XIV, XVI, and XVIII returned broad because a
  topology with Hands-positive or Flow-positive does not adequately capture
  the excluded Indigenous population as a Hands-negative signal — or, in later
  eras, the tension between material prosperity and unresolved political
  legitimacy. Era XVIII (2008–2013) also had broad because the Rudd/Gillard
  era's minority-government instability is not captured by an otherwise
  functional-positive topology.

CORE — USA (15 eras, 0 broad)
  USA returned 15/15 close. The USA's broad historical-source coverage and the
  CAMS topology's fit to the American political economy (strong Helm, Shield,
  Flow vectors) produced consistent mood-topology alignment across the full range
  from antebellum through COVID. No close calls; the USA is the most internally
  coherent match in CORE.

EXTENSION — China (20 eras, 1 broad)
  China returned 19/20 close. The exception was Era XX (2020–2025) where the
  Craft-led topology correctly captures export-manufacturing continuity but
  Shield's operational dominance during zero-COVID enforcement is not reflected
  in the leading nodes. All pre-PRC eras (I–XI, all-negative runs) produced
  close grades because the CAMS topology correctly identifies the dominant
  functional energy in each catastrophic or transitional era. The Great Leap
  Forward (XIII, Shield-only-positive) and Cultural Revolution (XV,
  Shield-only-positive) both returned close because Shield-only-positive is
  an exact structural description of PLA dominance during those eras. The
  one broad case (XX) was graded popular-partial because zero-COVID generated
  significant public dissatisfaction (November 2022 protests) not captured by
  an all-positive Craft-led topology.

EXTENSION — UK (16 eras, 1 broad)
  UK returned 15/16 close. The exception was Era VII (1946–1951, Attlee
  welfare state) where Hands-marginal topology creates a register gap: the
  Attlee government was elected on a historic labour-class mandate (1945
  landslide) and the popular mood would not have experienced welfare-state
  delivery as a Hands-marginal event, even if the bureaucratic mechanism
  (Archive leading) is structurally correct. UK's most consistent structural
  feature — Archive leading in all 16 eras — was consistently supported by
  historical evidence; this is a genuine and rare institutional fingerprint.

EXTENSION — Norway (15 eras, 3 broad)
  Norway's three broad cases (XII, XIII, XV) all share the same structural
  cause: Shield-sole-negative in eras when Norwegian defence commitments were
  publicly salient. Era XII (2005–2013): Norwegian troops in Afghanistan while
  Shield is structurally negative. Era XIII (2014–2019): post-Crimea NATO
  pressure and acknowledged defence gap while Shield-negative. Era XV
  (2022–2025): Ukraine war driving explicit Norwegian rearmament while
  Shield-negative. The topology correctly captures Norway's chronic under-
  investment in military capacity relative to GDP, but the official register
  of all three eras was Shield-activating in ways the topology does not reflect.
  Era XIV (COVID) returned close because pandemic management was genuinely
  Archive-led and civilian in register.

LIMIT — Denmark (19 eras, 6 broad, 1 partial, era IX omitted)
  Denmark had the highest broad rate of any entity (6/19 = 31.6%) and the only
  partial grade in the full dataset (Era VII).

  Era VII (1864–1873, partial): The 1864 defeat to Prussia was the defining
  national trauma of modern Danish history. All-positive Craft/Hands topology
  for an era opened by the loss of Schleswig-Holstein cannot match the popular
  emotional register of the first decade of this period. The Hedeselskabet
  recovery orientation (Craft/Hands-led internal development) is correctly
  captured, but the pocket does not distinguish the defeat-trauma phase from
  the recovery phase within a 10-year era.

  Broad cases by structural cause:
  — Eras III–IV (1807–1813, 1814–1839): identical pocket to era II masks the
    1807 British bombardment and 1814 Norway cession respectively.
  — Era VIII (1874–1900): Helm-positive does not capture Estrup's provisional-
    law regime (1877–1894), where Helm exercised power against parliamentary
    consent.
  — Era XV (1953–1971): Shield co-leads with Archive, but popular register
    experienced the era as welfare-state rather than military.
  — Eras XVIII–XIX (2002–2012, 2013–2019): Lore-marginal topology does not
    capture the Mohammed cartoon controversy (2005) or the refugee-crisis
    narrative contestation (2015).

  Era IX omission: by design. No pocket in Arm A, no record generated.

4. POPULAR / INSTITUTIONAL / OFFICIAL MOOD DIFFERENCES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Across all 126 eras, two systematic patterns emerged in mood-level differentiation:

(a) Official ahead of popular: The most common disaggregation pattern. Official
    registers frequently align with CAMS topology when the state is generating
    confident political rhetoric, while popular mood is partial or disagree.
    Examples: China XIII (Great Leap Forward) where official confidence was high
    but popular mood partial (forced compliance, private understanding of
    famine); China XV (Cultural Revolution) similarly; Russia XXIII (official
    propaganda active while popular mood is at best partial); China XVII
    (post-Tiananmen: official confidence, popular compliance but not full
    agreement). In these cases, positive-topology nodes (especially Helm and
    Shield) carry official activation without popular participation.

(b) Institutional-only alignment in bureaucratic eras: Several eras returned
    institutional-agree when popular and official were partial. Australia's
    colonial eras (IV, XI) and UK Era VII (Attlee) show Archive-led institutional
    functioning that is structurally correct but does not map to popular
    activation. The bureaucratic topology (Archive leading) can be accurate
    institutionally while substantially misrepresenting popular mood.

(c) Official-disagree relative to topology is rare: Only Denmark Era VII
    (1864 defeat) returned official-partial (not disagree). Official mood in
    post-defeat Denmark partially agreed with the recovery orientation but
    partially acknowledged the defeat trauma. No "official disagree" grade was
    returned in the dataset — official registers tend to validate CAMS topologies
    more consistently than popular registers, because CAMS nodes measure
    institutional function rather than popular affect.

(d) Partial popular, agree institutional/official is the modal disaggregation:
    The most common pattern for broad-graded eras. The Arm A pocket captures
    institutional and official function but misses something about popular affect
    or excluded populations. This pattern appears in Australia, China XVII,
    China XX, UK XI (Thatcher popular bifurcation), Norway XII–XV.

5. SOURCE LIMITATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

All sources cited in Arm C records were Wikipedia or Britannica pages accessed
2026-11-07. Limitations:

(a) Retrospective dominance: All web sources are retrospective histories, not
    contemporary evidence. The protocol permits web research in Arm C but requires
    distinguishing retrospective from contemporary evidence; all sources used here
    are retrospective. This means that mood assessments are based on the consensus
    historical record rather than contemporaneous surveys, diaries, newspapers, or
    political records.

(b) Anglocentric coverage: Pre-20th century evidence for China (I–XI), Denmark
    (I–VIII), and Norway (I–VI) is thinner in English-language sources. CAMS nodes
    for these eras rely on well-established historical events (Taiping Rebellion,
    Opium Wars, Danish-Prussian Wars, Norwegian Union dissolution) that are
    reasonably well-documented in English Wikipedia, but contemporary domestic
    primary sources were not consulted.

(c) China: English-language sources on PRC eras (XII–XX) carry a geopolitical
    vantage bias (predominantly Western sources). For the Great Leap Forward and
    Cultural Revolution, mortality estimates and institutional breakdown are well-
    documented. For recent eras (XVII–XX), popular mood assessments that agree
    with official topology require qualification: Western-sourced histories may
    understate genuine domestic popular support for the CCP.

(d) Russia (CORE): Sources used for all Russian eras were Wikipedia summaries.
    Russian-language primary sources were not consulted.

(e) Australia (CORE): Indigenous Australian mood and experience is poorly
    represented in standard historical sources. The partial/disagree grades for
    popular mood in several Australian eras reflect the absence of adequate
    primary evidence for Indigenous Australian perspectives rather than evidence
    of disagreement.

(f) Long eras: Several eras span 20–30 years (Denmark IV, XV; Norway VII–VIII).
    A single grade for a long era averages across turning points that the pocket
    also averages. Mood assessments for long eras carry greater uncertainty than
    for shorter eras.

6. DENMARK LIMITS (IDENTICAL SIGMA VECTORS)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Denmark presents a methodological challenge documented in Arm B and confirmed
in Arm C: several consecutive era groups have byte-identical Arm A pockets.

Identical groups and their extent:
  Eras III–IV:    Craft/Stewards pair, Hands/Helm/Shield positive; Archive marginal
  Eras V–VIII:    Craft/Hands pair, all positive; Archive marginal
  Eras X–XII:     Craft/Hands pair, Lore marginal, all positive
  Eras XVI–XVII:  Craft leads, Hands/Flow/Stewards positive; Shield marginal
  Eras XVIII–XX:  Craft/Archive pair, Flow/Stewards/Helm positive; Lore marginal

This means Arm C cannot use the pocket to distinguish between eras within an
identical group. For Arm C, the grade must reflect whether the identical
topology is consistent with each era's historical evidence independently.

Consequences observed:
  — Eras V–VIII: despite identical topology, era VII (1864 defeat) returned
    partial because the defeat event creates a register that all-positive topology
    cannot capture, even though eras V, VI, and VIII returned close.
  — Eras III–IV: both returned broad, but for different reasons (British
    bombardment 1807 for III; loss of Norway 1814 for IV). The identical pocket
    is a problem in both cases because it cannot encode either shock.
  — Eras X–XII: all returned close, the identical pocket fitting each era's
    neutral-wartime/Depression/Social-Democratic topology in sequence.
  — Eras XVI–XVII: both close (EEC integration era continuously fitting
    Craft-led topology).
  — Eras XVIII–XX: XVIII broad (cartoon controversy), XIX broad (refugee crisis),
    XX close. The identical Lore-marginal topology for eras XVIII–XIX missed two
    of the most significant public-discourse events in modern Danish history.

The identical sigma vector problem limits Arm C's discrimination for Denmark:
the pocket is a floor constraint, not a full description. Arm C can only note
where the identical topology is correct or incorrect for each era independently.

7. SUCCESSES AND FAILURES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SUCCESSES (eras where the CAMS topology is exceptionally well-validated by
independent historical evidence):

  China III (Taiping Rebellion): ALL-NEG with Hands/Shield least degraded is
  an unusually precise structural description of one of history's most
  destructive conflicts.

  China XIII (Great Leap Forward): Shield-only-positive is an exact description
  of the institutional reality — the PLA was the only functioning node while
  Archive (statistical falsification), Hands (famine deaths), and Flow
  (circulation collapse) were all severely negative.

  China XV (Cultural Revolution): Shield-only-positive again, with Archive
  explicitly the deepest negative (universities closed, records burned) —
  an exact structural description.

  Denmark XIII (Occupation): Lore-leads-Shield-negative is structurally exact
  for the Danish occupation — resistance narrative as the dominant positive
  activation and military capability as the defining negative.

  Norway VI (Occupation): Lore-leads-Helm/Craft/Shield-negative is equally
  exact for Norwegian occupation topology.

  Russia/USSR eras generally: Russia returned only 2 broad in 23 eras, the
  lowest broad rate in CORE. The CAMS topology maps well onto Russian
  institutional dynamics across the full range from Tsarist through Soviet to
  post-Soviet.

  UK Archive dominance (all 16 eras): The finding that Archive leads in every
  UK era from 1880 to 2025 is consistently validated by independent historical
  evidence. British institutional continuity — the sustained strength of law,
  bureaucracy, and administrative record-keeping — is a genuine structural
  feature that CAMS correctly identifies and that Arm C independently confirms.

FAILURES AND WEAKNESSES:

  Australia colonial-racial structure: The CAMS topology systematically under-
  encodes the colonial-racial structure as a stress variable. Hands-positive in
  eras where Indigenous Australians are excluded from the labour market, and
  Flow-positive in eras sustained by colonial extraction, are structurally
  incomplete. This is the most systematic weakness in the CORE dataset.

  Denmark identical-pocket eras and shock events: The identical pocket for eras
  III–IV fails to encode the British bombardment (1807) and loss of Norway
  (1814) respectively. The identical pocket for eras XVIII–XIX fails to encode
  the Mohammed cartoon controversy and refugee crisis. These are not random
  misses — they reflect a consistent pattern where long eras with identical
  pockets cannot capture within-era shock events.

  Lore/Shield tension in occupied and coercive states: Where a state uses
  Lore (propaganda, cultural production) as a coercive tool, the CAMS topology's
  Lore node generates a tension: structural Lore-negative (institutional decay)
  vs official Lore-positive (propaganda output). This was noted in Russia XXIII
  and implicitly in China XIII–XV. The Arm A pocket correctly identifies the
  structural condition; Arm C grades these as broad when the official register
  is primarily propaganda-driven rather than genuine cultural activation.

  Norway Shield: The consistent Shield-negative finding for Norway 2005–2025
  generates a tension with actual NATO deployment and rearmament rhetoric.
  Three consecutive broad grades (XII, XIII, XV) all share this cause. This
  suggests the Shield node in the CAMS topology is measuring structural
  military capacity rather than political defence rhetoric, which is correct
  — but creates a systematic gap with official registers in NATO member states
  that publicly declare defence priorities they have not yet funded.
