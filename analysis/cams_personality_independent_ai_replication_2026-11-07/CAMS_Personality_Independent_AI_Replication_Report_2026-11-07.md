## 2. What was tested

The claim under test is the one written in [`evidence/experimental_plan.md`](evidence/experimental_plan.md) on 6 November 2026:

> Eight CAMS node functions, read only through Cog=A×C, Aff=K−S, σ=Cog×Aff with the compact pocket rules, produce era personalities recognisable against sealed historical eras when written by AI systems that never see the original analyst pockets or mood grades.

In words: each node has an abstraction A, a capacity C, a knowledge K, and a stress S. Cognitive focus is the product A×C. Affect, or available energy, is K−S. The pocket signal σ is the product of those two. Juno is not part of this round. The value formula and the bond formula used elsewhere in this repository were not the instruments under test. Grok’s Arm A ledger says the pockets were written with no V and no bond.

The eight names used in the sealed pockets are Helm, Shield, Lore, Stewards, Craft, Hands, Archive, and Flow. This repository’s existing public description maps those functions to governance, security, knowledge, resource stewardship, production, labour, memory, and exchange. The standard-packet node manual itself is not in the attachment, so this report does not restate rules that were not deposited.

A pocket names who is leading and who is constrained, in functional language, without the historical title of the era. Arm B asks whether that portrait is enough to pick the era from a closed list. Arm C asks whether the same portrait still agrees with the historical mood once web sources are allowed, with popular feeling, organised institutional mood, and official messaging kept separate.

### 3.4 Pre-registered criteria, and the line between a number and a protocol

From [`evidence/experimental_plan.md`](evidence/experimental_plan.md), the pitch bar was:

- Arm A: at least 60% leadership-pair and constraint match on Russia, Australia, and the USA, across at least three models.
- Arm B: at least twice the chance hit rate. Denmark may fail without sinking the core result.
- Arm C: a majority of Q3 grades exact or adjacent, and no overturning disagreement pattern.
- Nulls fall to chance.

Arm A’s 60% figure was never computed in the files that were attached. This report does not supply one.

Arm B chance, as defined in the score pack, is the mean of the per-row values `1 / (number of eras on that society’s shortlist)`. “Twice chance” means the hit rate is at least twice that mean. For every scored CORE row the stored chance averages 0.053572, which the sealed summary prints as 5.36%, so twice chance is 10.71%. That comparison is the pre-registered rule. It is not a significance test, and none was added.

Arm C adjacency is defined by the READY stub, written before any Arm C return was scored ([`evidence/seals/armC_scores_READY/README.md`](evidence/seals/armC_scores_READY/README.md), packet ledger 2026-11-06 23:59 AEDT):

- Exact: the returned grade equals the sealed grade.
- Adjacent, and only these pairs: close↔broad, broad↔partial, partial↔mixed.
- Not adjacent: every other mismatch. In particular, mixed↔direct_disagreement is not adjacent, and close↔partial is not adjacent.

An overturning disagreement is a tester grade of `direct_disagreement` where the sealed grade was `close` or `broad`. A sealed `direct_disagreement` that comes back as `close` or `broad` is a different event. The READY stub says to inspect those reversals. It does not rename them as overturns. The Gemini audit addendum keeps that distinction, and so does this report.

A majority for a set means more than half the scored rows in that set. The sealed summaries apply that as at least 29/56 on CORE, at least 26/51 on EXTENSION, and at least 10/19 on Denmark.

Protocol status is separate from that arithmetic. A set can meet the numeric bar and still fail as a cryptographically verified, pre-sealed replication. Section 8 is that separation. No headline in this report upgrades a descriptive numeric pass into a clean protocol pass.

### 3.5 What “lenient” means here, without a chance model for Arm C

No Arm C chance baseline was sealed, and this report does not invent one. The reason a majority exact-or-adjacent result is a weak specificity screen is visible in the grades themselves.

On CORE, both models returned only `close` and `broad`: Claude 48 close and 8 broad; Gemini 52 close and 4 broad. The sealed CORE margin, identical in both score packs, is 28 close, 17 broad, 6 partial, 5 mixed, and 0 direct disagreement. Two neighbouring grades, which are also the two most common sealed grades, will often count as exact-or-adjacent even when the return is systematically more generous than the seal. The non-exact ledger is mostly sealed `broad` returned as `close`. That pattern is reported. It is not converted into a p-value.
