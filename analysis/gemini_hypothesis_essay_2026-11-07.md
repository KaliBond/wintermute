A short essay on the eight-node portrait. 7 November 2026.

The name comes from an outline titled “The Gemini hypothesis”. That outline was supplied as Claude input. The filename does not decide who wrote it. This essay keeps the useful proposals in that outline and ties them to the sealed replication report. It does not treat the proposals as results.

## The proposal

Eight functions, read as Cog = A × C and Aff = K − S, with σ = Cog × Aff, give a short portrait of a collective at one time. The ambitious hypothesis is that this portrait is not a story about the collective. It is a compressed signature of how the collective is coordinating, and that the same lean set is the minimum a coordinating system needs, whether the system is a state or a firm. On that hypothesis the relevant clock is institutional memory and generational learning, not a single headline.

Culture, narrative, and markets would then be ways the signature is expressed, not a substitute for it. That is the bet. The replication does not establish it, and it does not show that σ is a measured physical rate of entropy or that a weak node is a fall toward thermodynamic equilibrium. Those readings can stay on the table as hypotheses. They are not findings.

## Two tests, two claims

The closed round asked two different questions.

Arm B asked whether a pocket is informationally dense: can a reader pick the era from a closed list, with no web and no titles? That is a recognition test.

Arm C asked whether the same pocket agrees with a historical mood, once web sources are allowed and popular feeling, institutional mood, and official messaging are kept apart. That is a mood test. It is not a second copy of the recognition test, and it is not a causal test of behaviour.

Exact agreement, an adjacent grade, and “close” are three different things. Adjacent means only close with broad, broad with partial, and partial with mixed. Mixed and direct disagreement are not neighbours. A majority of exact-or-adjacent grades is a coarse screen, especially where almost every return sits on close or broad.

## What the core returns actually did

On the core set, Russia, Australia, and the USA, 56 eras, blind recognition was strong for some models and not for others. Claude and Gemini each matched 56/56. Kimi matched 52/56. Grok matched 19/56, which still clears the pre-registered bar of twice the shortlist chance rate. Codex matched 3/56 and does not clear it. Manus matched 56/56 and is flagged: Arm A had already been read in the same session, so that figure is not a clean blind. Details and the extension and Denmark rows are in the report. Extension recognition was much weaker where it was run.

On mood, the core figures that were scored and checked are these. Claude: 27/56 exact and 48/56 exact-or-adjacent. Gemini: 27/56 exact and 46/56 exact-or-adjacent. Gemini’s numbers describe the received files only. The claimed Arm A and Arm C hashes were not computed over those files. Claude’s output hashes match the received Arm C files. The Arm A file those rows name was not in the deposit.

A single “percent close” across all 126 eras is not used here. Exact is not adjacent, and a pooled percentage is not the core result.

Denmark is the sparse-input control. Identical input rows can force identical pockets. That makes some eras hard to tell apart. It does not make Denmark a perfect control, and it does not mean Denmark was required to fail. The Denmark mood returns are weaker than the core, and they are reported separately in the sealed packs, including sealed disagreements that came back as close or broad.

The models are not independent witnesses. The era means were coded with history in view, and a model can know a period without being shown its name. Blind, in this design, means the titles, the grades, and the key were withheld. It does not mean the portrait was built from eight numbers that had never touched a history.

## Firms, as a proposal

The same algebra is already used on firms in the ADVERSARY-02 scoring prompts. Those prompts list firm nodes in the order Board, Risk, RD, Finance, Operations, Workforce, Brand, Markets, beside the national order Helm, Shield, Lore, Stewards, Craft, Hands, Archive, Flow. A correspondence that begins Helm with Board, Shield with Risk, and Lore with RD follows that order. It is a working list in those prompts. It is not a proof that the functions are the same thing, and this essay does not add firm-era numbers to it.

A bridge study would score the same cases once under the national coding and once under the firm coding and compare them. That study has not been run. This essay does not authorise it.

If the portrait travels, one instrument can be used at both scales without changing the algebra. If it does not travel, the failure is informative: some functions a state carries may be amplified or missing inside a firm. Either outcome would teach more than the order of names does now.

## What would count against it

Three results would hit the hypothesis. None of them has been run as a decisive control, so it is not available to say that falsification was attempted and failed.

1. On a held-out set, mood grades from the portrait do no better than a stated base rate.
2. Era recognition falls to the chance rate once the test controls for which society is being read, rather than which era inside it.
3. The firm mapping produces diagnostics that do not travel: the same algebra, applied under the firm node list, does not yield a portable portrait.

Until those are done, the live claim is narrower and already concrete. A lean eight-node portrait was enough, for the stronger models, to recognise core eras from a blinded pocket at a rate above the pre-registered bar, and enough to show a descriptive mood agreement that has to be read with the hash and grading caveats in the report. That is a promising instrument demonstration. It is not peer review, and it is not a causal theory of history.
