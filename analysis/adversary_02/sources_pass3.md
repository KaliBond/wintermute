## Thin evidence / NA notes

**China:** No NAs (the v2.1 rubric does not allow them). The evidence is weakest for Archive (court and bureaucratic performance is opaque) and for Hands 2013–2019 (labour unrest data stopped being collected after China Labour Bulletin coverage became patchy). Shield scores rest on headline events (purges, reform) rather than any read on readiness.

**Evergrande (19 NA):** RD is NA 2010–2018. Before 2019 the diversification was announcements and capital commitment, with no demonstrated knowledge output in construction technology. RD 2019–2020 is scored low because the evidence is acquisitions, prototypes and the Faraday dispute, with no production. Workforce is NA 2010–2019: by the cutoffs there was no concrete public indicator of labour or talent strain (the employee-WMP revelations date from Sep 2021, after the cutoff). Workforce 2020 rests on the Sep 2020 staff-wide discount-sales mobilisation. Operations delivery figures depend on Evergrande's own annual reports, and independent completion data was thin.

**Vanke (9 NA):** RD is NA 2010–2014 (industrialised housing research existed, but there was no concrete performance or strain indicator by the cutoffs). Workforce is NA 2010–2013 (no specific strain indicator before the 2014 partner scheme). Risk 2010–2014 and Markets carry near-identical scores, kept after re-checking because Vanke's leverage and controls were stable. Those repeats are a real finding, not carry-forward.

**Longfor (24 NA):** RD is NA 2010–2015 (Paradise Walk is a format, not demonstrable R&D output). Workforce is NA 2010–2017 and 2019. Risk is NA 2013–2017 (no concrete strain indicator, and conservatism alone does not pass the gate). Brand is NA 2010–2013. Longfor is the thinnest-covered developer in English-language sources, so these scores rest mainly on annual reports and HKEX sales announcements.

**Huawei (3 NA):** Workforce is NA 2010–2012 (TUP detail and strain indicators are disclosed from ~2013). Operations 2010–2014 has only a thin strain indicator. Finance relies wholly on self-published audited reports. For Risk 2018–2020 I separated the internal compliance questions raised by the indictment (Skycom) from external sanctions pressure, and scored Stress only on the internal part.

**SMIC (21 NA):** Risk is NA 2013–2018 (no concrete strain or performance indicator for the compliance function between the post-TSMC-settlement period and the 2019–2020 US actions). Workforce is NA 2010, 2013–2016 and 2018–2019. Brand (customer trust) is NA 2010, 2012–2018, because customer-level evidence is mostly private. For 2020 Risk I scored internal function only, and kept the entity listing as external. The Board 2020 drop rests on the leaked Liang resignation letter (internal), not on the listing.

**BYD (12 NA):** Risk is NA 2014, 2017 and 2019 (no concrete compliance or safety indicator recalled for those years). Workforce is NA 2010 and 2012–2019 (only headcount was available, which the rubric excludes). 2011 rests on reported restructuring and staff cuts, and 2020 on the mask-line redeployment.

**CATL (44 NA):** 2011–2014 are all NA. CATL was private, its first prospectus (FY2015–2017) only appeared in 2018, and each year's 30 June Y+1 cutoff excludes that later disclosure. 2015–2016 is scored only where contemporaneous industry data existed (installation rankings for Operations and Markets, and the 2016 NCM transition for RD). Workforce 2017 is NA (no strain indicator in the prospectus that I can recall with confidence).

## Limitations

1. **Hindsight contamination risk is high, especially for Evergrande, SMIC and Huawei.** I know about Evergrande's 2021 default, SMIC's later 7nm work and Huawei's 2021–2023 trajectory. I tried to score 2019–2020 only on in-cutoff evidence: the Three Red Lines, the Guangdong letter and the 2020 annual report for Evergrande; the Dec 2020 listings for SMIC; the Sep 2020 TSMC cutoff for Huawei. Even so, the early-year Evergrande Risk and Finance scores (Stress 6–7 from 2012) may be harsher than a 2013 observer would have scored them. The Citron 2012 report is real in-period evidence, but the weight I gave it may be shaped by what happened later.
2. Every source is recalled from memory and none was verified. Dates are approximate, and a few figures (exact profits, sales, market shares) may be off by a modest margin.
3. Strict application of the evidence gate yields high NA rates for private or thinly covered entities (CATL, Longfor, SMIC customer data). This reflects how little could be observed at the time, not whether the function actually existed.
4. Some scores repeat from year to year (e.g. Vanke Markets, Longfor Finance, Huawei Workforce). Each repeat was kept after a re-check, but with memory-only evidence that re-check has limited resolution.
5. The China (v2.1) scores mix pass/fail headline events with structural judgement. Without NA, the thin-evidence nodes (Archive, Hands) carry more scorer prior than data.
6. Western coverage of these entities runs adversarial. I tried to score internal function (e.g. Huawei RD and Operations under sanctions, SMIC delivery) on its own terms and to treat geopolitical pressure as external, as the rubric requires.
