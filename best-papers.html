Prospective and pre-registered tests

The only tests that can’t be gamed retrospectively. Ranked first because the design does the defending.

1

PR-GAP-2: CAMS as a Leading Indicator of Societal Rupture

Pre-registeredFinding Updated Nov 2026 · 44 societies, 1900–2025, 126 ruptures

Leave-one-society-out cross-validation reaches AUC 0.740 for deficit crises once coordination transitions are correctly separated: using only structural coordination metrics, no political or economic covariates.

Why first: the strongest single empirical claim on the site: genuine out-of-sample structure, an explicit confound-handling move, and a headline number modest enough to believe. Read beside the full paper next.

2

PR-GAP-2: Structural Coordination and Rupture Morphology, Full Paper

Pre-registeredValidation Aug 2026 draft · with findings summary and six supporting analyses

The full academic paper behind the rupture study: panel validation across 44 societies, Robson Gauge cultural-grounding results, and a cross-system parsimony assessment. Supported by the findings note, a synthesis, a formalism audit, an NCT/LOSO report, a Robson-plane analysis and a general-audience version.

Why second: the closest thing CAMS has to a flagship manuscript, and it carries its own audit trail in public.

3

ADVERSARY-02 Results: a Pre-registered Test of CAMS

Pre-registered 6 Nov 2026 · 15 predictions, China + 7 firms, 2021–2024

Fifteen predictions sealed before any scoring: six passed, nine failed, and the built-in kill-switch (FC5) was not triggered. Written for new readers, with the scoreboard, scorer grades and limitations.

Why third: the cleanest demonstration of the project’s epistemic discipline, a 40% hit rate published with the same prominence as a win would get. That is what pre-registration is for.

4

Persistent National Topology in Coded Institutional Profiles

Pre-registeredBlind rounds 15 Nov 2026 · four blind rounds, 24 eras

Four pre-registered blind rounds asking whether a national signature persists in CAMS 8×4 profiles across 24 eras. Crisis-swamping and single-carrier explanations are falsified; Helm is flagged as σ’s blind spot.

Why fourth: a falsification-oriented design that reports what didn’t work and names the instrument’s own weak node, rarer and more valuable than another confirmation.

5

Predictions on Record: the 2027–2028 Register

Pre-registered Lodged Aug 2026 · 32 claims · scored when the years close

Thirty-two probability-weighted claims about the USA, China and Australia for 2027–2028, lodged at Zenodo and frozen. Currently the strongest reason to wait before judging the framework.

Why fifth: not a result but the decisive pending experiment, the entry that turns every other ranking on this page into a provisional one.

Validation studies and blind rounds

Does the instrument measure what it claims? Ranked by design quality and by how much of the framework each one stress-tests.

6

Graph-Theoretic Diagnosis of Societal Collapse: CAMS Validation Across 18 Societies

Validation 10 Jun 2026 · 10–2026 CE · 12 falsifiable predictions

Tests three core CAMS claims against 18 societies spanning 2,000 years: dimensional collapse, node coupling architectures, and crisis-signature matching, with 12 predictions stated up front.

Why sixth: the broadest historical validation and an early statement of the falsification culture that later work sharpened.

7

CAMS Temporal Inelasticity: Results

Validation 15 Aug 2026 · internal audit · Cuba, Iceland, ASPI pilots

Shows the JUNO v1.2 bond formula factors completely into node-level weights: B = w⊗w is rank-1, so the bond matrix carries no pair-specific information and λ₂ is algebraically constrained rather than empirically discovered.

Why seventh, and arguably first in importance: the most consequential single result on the site; it defunds an entire family of “network” claims. A model auditing itself this hard earns the right to be read.

8

JUNO/CAMS Blind-Analysis Protocol v1.1.1

Blind rounds Nov 2026 · 24 societies, 3 blind sets

Results of the cognitive-affective extension experiment: MRR improved 0.667 → 0.833, but the calibration gate failed on conflict states.

Why eighth: an honest mixed result that marks its own boundary, exactly where a blind protocol earns trust.

9

JUNO/CAMS Blind Analysis Challenge

Blind rounds Open now · 8 anonymised societies, 1750–2026

The open challenge: load an anonymised dataset and the protocol prompt into any capable LLM, run the five-stage analysis, and compare against the sealed key before unblinding.

Why ninth: not a finding but an invitation, the entry point for any outsider who wants the strongest form of evidence the project can offer: their own run.

An Accumulating Case for CAMS: the Personality Experiment

Blind rounds 7 Nov 2026 · recognition counts start closed

A possible unification of meaning and metabolism, offered with the mechanism explicitly not treated as demonstrated: and a participant route that keeps the November grades undisclosed so you can take part clean.

Why tenth: the most methodologically careful piece of speculative work on the site: it builds a disclosure firewall into its own design. The speculation is labelled speculation.

Ensemble Validation: CAMS v2.3

Validation May 2026 · Germany & USA 1880–2026

The Wright Thesis confirmed with qualifications: variance reduction, SNR improvement, contestation index and κ-uncertainty bounds for ensemble scoring.

Operator Portability in CAMS: Synthesis & Verification Trail

Validation Nov 2026 · two independent computational audits

Canonical operators are structurally portable across corporate and national domains but parametrically non-invariant: confirmed twice independently. The companion Soil-Bond Index paper is explicitly paused pending rework.

Why twelfth: the cross-scale claim tested with replication, plus a rare public example of a result being stood down rather than quietly left standing.

13

P4 Retrospective Validation: Does the Crisis Detector Work on Firms?

Validation 10 Nov 2026 · interim · 3 cases, 6 matched controls

A rule sealed on fifteen national crises was run unchanged on Boeing, Theranos and WorldCom against matched survivors. The controls set it off almost as often as the cases.

Why thirteenth: a clean negative that bounds the portability claim, published as prominently as the positives. This is the audit culture working.

Why fourteenth: a whole paper about not over-claiming the interpretive layer; methodologically interesting beyond CAMS.

Findings from the datasets

What the numbers say, once the instrument’s limits are on the table. In-sample unless marked; the distinction is marked throughout.

17

The Synchronised Slide: Global Stress After 2000

Finding 23 Oct 2026 · JUNO v3 common-factor analysis · external series: IMF, World Bank, NASA GISTEMP, FRED

Stress synchrony jumps after 2000 to war-era levels without a world war, carried by a new near-uniform global factor. Of the drivers tested, only the public-debt build-up tracks it; the 1920s–30s parallel arrives with the rate sign inverted.

Why seventeenth: the most scientifically interesting descriptive finding, an unexpected, testable macro claim with the comparison series named, and the inverted-sign parallel flagged rather than smoothed over.

18

Organisational Pathologies: Interim Results

Finding 20 Oct 2026 · six frozen signatures · 37 JUNO v3 societies

The failures we recognise in families and offices recur at national scale: twelve of eighteen named historical windows hold, all four windowed signatures cluster beyond chance, and Late Abstraction Collapse precedes big falls.

Why eighteenth: the cross-scale claim that would matter most if it survives replication, and “twelve of eighteen” is reported as twelve of eighteen.

19

US Legitimacy Crisis × CAMS Stress: Study 2

Finding Nov 2026 · 1996–2025 panel

CAMS Stress leads Gallup and Pew institutional-trust series by 2–3 years: the exact opposite of the fiscal finding in Study 1.

Why nineteenth: a lead-lag result against external survey data is genuinely testable, and the open contradiction with Study 1 is the interesting part; two probes, two answers, both published.

Attractor Coherence, Revisited

An old experiment re-run on the combined JUNO dataset after its first blind prospective test failed: do societies break in the same places every time?

Why twentieth: valuable as candour, a failed replication revisited on fuller data, with the “not blind” label worn on the sleeve.
