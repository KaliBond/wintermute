The USA series against conventional history

Two pre-registered tests on the annual USA ensemble: period profiles against blind raters, and drift against external markers

Kari McKern · ComplexityWorkz · 29 October 2026

Both tests were frozen before they were run. Every number on this page comes from the run logs and CSV files in the repository folder linked below. The panel reads panel_data.json from that folder.

Summary

  1. Study 1: ensemble against 25 periods. Period node profiles from the annual USA ensemble agree with the blind-rater consensus (H1 supported) and the three named transitions move the same way in both sources, with all eight nodes matching on each (H3 supported). H2, that relational drift begins before the conventional transition years, is not supported: 5 of 14 transitions had a drift upcrossing in the five years before them, against 6 of 14 in the four years from the date onward.
  2. The reading. Conventional history dates are themselves coordination-stress signals: wars, crashes and realignments get dated because strain has become visible. A drift measure should then rise at or after those dates, and H2 as drafted could not separate the two. A miss on H2 is not a miss for the series.
  3. Study 2: external markers. Study 2 swapped the conventional dates for dates set outside the author's judgement (NBER cycle peaks as the primary set, with party-control switches and amendment ratifications as secondary sets, and census years as a negative control). Drift upcrossings did not lead NBER peaks: 3 of 19 against 3 lagging, chance p = 0.75. Not supported. Only 4 drift upcrossings exist in the state-based series, so the test could not have been decisive either way.
  4. The census control was clean (3 of 11, p = 0.37), so the null result is not a calendar artefact.

The frozen rule asked for a lead share of at least 0.60 on the primary set, more lead than lag hits, and both p-values at or below 0.05. NBER peaks met none of these. Five of the pre-registered failure criteria triggered: F1 (lead share 0.158), F2 (lead hits not above lag hits), F3 (both p-values above 0.05), F7 (the anchoring check: one upcrossing, 1981, sits on a marker year) and F8 (fewer than five drift upcrossings, so the test is declared uninformative). Under the annual drift X (20 peaks fall in its range) the lead hits are 3 against 7 lag hits (pN1 = 1.00). Neither secondary set passed the Holm correction.

Exploratory runs carry no verdict. NBER troughs: 4 of 19 lead, 1 lag (p = 0.43). Presidency party switches alone: 4 of 12 lead (p = 0.12), the smallest p among the exploratory sets and still well above 0.05. Lower and higher thresholds (85th and 95th percentile) move the NBER lead count to 4 and 1.

What the two studies say together

The series carries the same node-level shape as the blind period profiles and the same direction of change across the three named transitions. That is the part Study 1 was built to test, and it passed on both annual files. What neither study shows is drift beginning ahead of dated events. With conventional dates the result is confounded in the way described above. With external dates the test had four events to work with.

The next useful step is a longer or finer series, since the state-based drift needs changes in the scored state to fire and this file changes state in blocks: 54 distinct annual states in 126 years, and 72 of 125 year-to-year changes are exactly zero. Lead timing inside a block cannot be resolved.

Status and provenance

  • Frozen before running. Study 1 PREREG sha256 3603e474…aea2fe; Study 2 PREREG sha256 38dcbe91…04c5. Full hashes, the marker files and the run scripts are in the repository folder.
  • Dataset sequence. Study 1 was first run on the hi-fi annual file as a test, then on a five-yearly file, then on the annual USA ensemble file, which was named canonical after the first two results had been seen. Thresholds, year tables, nulls and seed (20261029) were not changed. This is logged in the deviation notes.
  • Annual file. The analysed copy has md5 1c9626e9481662df6c00974f720e0ec0. It was reconstructed from a text read of the supplied file, so there is no original checksum to compare. Run from a byte copy if exact provenance matters.
  • Period dates. The 15 periods and their year boundaries are the author's working definition from the casebook cue text. There is no sealed case-to-period map.
  • Node names follow JUNO v1.2: Helm, Shield, Lore, Stewards, Craft, Hands, Archive, Flow.
