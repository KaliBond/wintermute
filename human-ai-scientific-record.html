4. What the record argues

  1. Thermodynamic access. Cooperation widens what a group can reach, as a pack takes prey a lone animal cannot.
  2. Exteriorised memory. Tools and symbols carry skill and commitment outside the mind, so they can accumulate across generations.
  3. Functional organisation. Group cooperation rests on interdependent capabilities that recur as coordination roles. Eight roles is a modelling choice.
  4. Persistent state signalling. Organisations reveal their condition through what they do and record, which is why independent readings can repeat.
  5. Pathologies recur at every scale. Families, firms and states share structural requirements, so they can fail in similar ways.
  6. Non-moral evaluation. The subject is the structural health of cooperation, not a ranking of peoples.

The project's method is to accept AI ratings in good faith because they are repeatable, inspectable and contestable. The blind test supports repeatability on a shared casebook. The record keeps that claim separate from claims about historical truth.

5. Provenance

The hashes show that the ZIP and its contents are unchanged since the checksum was made. They don't establish authorship, dates, ChatGPT origin or the 30-month span; the certificate states this itself. The package recommends these steps to make the record authoritative:

  1. Export the full ChatGPT history through the account's data export, and hash it unedited.
  2. Sign the ZIP hash with a researcher-held key (Ed25519 or similar) and publish the public key.
  3. Anchor the hash in time: an RFC 3161 timestamp, OpenTimestamps, or a Zenodo deposit.
  4. Keep v1.0 frozen. Issue changes as new versions with their own hashes.
  5. Review the personal passages before making the record public.
