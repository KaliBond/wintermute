An accumulating case, and a way to test it

Distributed institutional coordination — memory, interpretation, direction, and action spread across an organisation — yields patterns of collective mood and meaning. Political narratives and events may partly express those deeper configurations. They are not assumed to be the whole of politics, and this page does not treat the mechanism as demonstrated.

The recognition counts start closed. The JUNO section that follows is already public, and it is a different series. The disclosure further down contains the November personality round’s headline grades. If you want to take part without those grades, use the participant route and do not open the disclosure, the replication report, or the historical Arm B pockets. This page is not the separate JUNO/CAMS Blind Analysis Challenge.

The case, in one place

Taken together, differentiated functional roles, patterned temporal relationships and recognisable historical moods give CAMS an accumulating explanatory case. They motivate the proposal that political expression partly emerges from deeper organisational configurations.

That proposal is not a proof. The scores are historically informed, including shared AI coding, so agreement among models is not an outside observation of institutions. A lag is not a cause. Recognising a historical mood is not an independent measure of cognition. The portrait does not treat a nation as a mind or a person.

Two instruments sit under that case, and they are not pooled. JUNO v3 dynamics use node value, V = C + K + A/2 − S. The personality experiment uses cognition A × C, affect K − S, and activation σ = cognition × affect. A result on one does not become a result on the other.

The journey through time

The substantive target is the narrative of an entity’s journey: how capacities, pressures and responses develop together, not a stack of separate scores and not a correct era name. Purely quantitative tests overlook that narrative. The diagnostics complement a historical reading. They do not replace it. A fluent story is not confirmation either. The November era-identification round does not already establish the journey. Matching one window is not a reconstruction of the sequence, the transitions, or the recovery and failure that join those windows.

JUNO v3: roles and timing

The rebuilt corpus is juno/v3/JUNO_Unified_Dataset_v3_2026-10-19.csv: 42 societies and 41,914 rows. It is not the older JUNO_36 explorer file, and it is not the personality era-means tables. The frozen signature run used 37 of the 42. Denmark, India, Turkey, Thailand and Canada were left out, and Israel was used only from 2015. Under thresholds frozen before the count, the rules fired in 696 society-years. Twelve of eighteen named windows still fired. Those windows were named from draft firings, so the enrichment shows that the pattern survives a stricter pre-set threshold. It is not a prospective discovery, and it is not a claim that the scores were blind to history.

The public write-up is Organisational Pathologies. The same counts are in juno/v3/CAMS_Thesis_v2_2026-10-20.md. One signature, Late Abstraction Collapse, is followed by a large fall in mean V more often than a circular-shift null: 62 against 38.56 expected. That is a timing result inside this corpus, not a cause. The companion thesis notes that part of the rule already contains the fall, because a drop in Coherence lowers V directly (docs/theory/CAMS_Organisational_Pathologies_Thesis.md).

The eight functions

These are the meanings in the issued node card. A portrait does not need every function to lead. A function can stand first because it is strong, or because the others are weaker.

  • Helm Executive direction and the capacity to decide.
  • Lore Shared explanation, legitimacy, and public speech.
  • Craft Making, technique, and organised skill.
  • Hands Everyday work at scale.
  • Flow Circulation and exchange.
  • Stewards Provisioning, maintenance, and care.
  • Shield Protection and coercive capacity. Read it against its neighbours: strong neighbours make it look like protection; weak or split neighbours make it look like coercion or protective custody.
  • Archive Record, procedure, and institutional memory.

The four dimensions, as issued

The participant packet scores each function on A Abstraction, C Coherence, K Capacity, and S Stress. The issued algebra then uses only three quantities:

Cognition = A × C
Affect = K − S
Activation (σ) = Cognition × Affect

Cognition is not the word “focus”. The reading rules treat focus and activation as separate readings. High focus with negative activation is strain, not health. Positive σ is not happiness, and σ is not a physical energy. The kit does not use node value or bond strength. Rank is a secondary check, not another mechanism.

The closed-round report’s plain-language gloss once calls C “capacity” and K “knowledge”, and it calls these products cognitive focus and available energy. That gloss is not the issued algebra. The formulas in the report’s own claim under test, Cog = A × C, Aff = K − S, and σ = Cog × Aff, match the packet. C is Coherence. K is Capacity.

The era-means tables were historically informed coding, including AI scoring. Withholding the episode title later does not remove that historical judgement. AI models also already contain historical text. Masking a title is not the same thing as a historically blank mind.

The three arms

Do them in order. Use a fresh session for each arm. Record the model name and version, and record the clock time you actually finished, with its time zone.

Arm A — write the portraits, no web

Read the era-means tables, the node definitions, and the pocket rules. For each era, name the leading and constraining functions and write a short functional portrait. The tables name the country and the years. They do not name the historical episode. That is the protocol that was issued, so Arm A is not geography-blind. Do not add titles, and do not compute node value or bond strength.

Arm B — identify the era, no web

In a fresh session that is not shown the era-means table, read a portrait and choose its era from a shortlist. Each portrait already names its country. The historical Arm B packet in this kit has already been used, and its results are public. It is a spoiled historical exercise, not a fresh blind test. A new blind Arm B needs new portraits and a new key, made by an operator after your Arm A file is hashed. The operator keeps that key. You do not.

Arm C — compare with the mood of the period, web allowed

Compare your own already hashed Arm A portraits with historical sources. Grade each era close, broad, partial, mixed, or direct_disagreement. Keep popular feeling, organised institutional mood, and official messaging separate. Do not edit, regenerate, or rehash the Arm A file. The original mood key is not public. Your own grades are descriptive. They do not count as the closed round’s central score. A scored mood test needs an operator to write a new key before you start, and to keep it off your machine.

How the case can fail

The question is whether this explanatory account survives independent comparisons, timing tests, and rival explanations. It is not only a question of whether eight is the right count of nodes. The tests below are proposals. They were not completed as a new preregistration, and this page adds no threshold.

  • A window list fixed before any firing, on a corpus that is not this one, does not concentrate the signatures beyond the baseline named in advance. The present 12 of 18 cannot be reused as that test: those windows were taken from draft firings.
  • A timing comparison fixed in advance shows no lead, or the lead vanishes once the rule is not allowed to share components with the outcome. Late Abstraction Collapse’s overlap with the fall is already a reason for caution, not a settled cause.
  • A rival account — a simpler index, a narrative baseline, or an economic series — matches the same windows and the same moods.
  • The journey, judged on its own, fails an independent historical reading. The proposed test fixes the rubric and the negative controls first, then compares sequences, transitions, and recovery or failure with rival narratives that had the same information. A fluent CAMS story would not count as a pass, and a quantitative hit would not replace that reading.
  • Held-out era recognition does no better than the baselines fixed before the run. That would weaken the mood strand. It would not, by itself, erase the JUNO signature record, and a JUNO failure would not erase a clean recognition result. Neither result is the journey. The series stay separate.
  • Readers who are not shown the function labels cannot reproduce the reading, or a mapping fixed in advance fails for firms or bureaucracies. Company means were not part of personality arms A–C.

Even a clean recognition result would not establish cognition. That still needs separate measures of organisational memory, learning, and action. A new blind identification needs new portraits and a new key held by an operator after the Arm A hash. A scored mood test needs a new key written before the participant starts. The closed plan named three nulls — shuffled σ, random means, and a Wikipedia-only condition — and no result file for them is in the deposit. This page does not supply one.

Core mood agreement

Only Claude and Gemini returned core Arm C. “Exact” means the returned grade equals the sealed grade. “Exact or adjacent” adds only these neighbour pairs: close with broad, broad with partial, and partial with mixed. Mixed with direct disagreement is not adjacent. Close with partial is not adjacent. Both models’ core returns used only close and broad. The sealed core margin, the same in both score packs, was 28 close, 17 broad, 6 partial, 5 mixed, and 0 direct disagreement. Two neighbouring grades, which are also the two most common sealed grades, will often count as exact-or-adjacent. The closed round’s numeric screen was a majority of the 56 rows, applied as at least 29/56. Meeting that screen is not independent historical truth.

The other rows’ claimed Arm A digest has no file in the deposit, so those digests stay unverified. Do not read 13/18 as a set of confirmed hashes.

Provenance

Gemini admitted that the SHA-256 values written in the Arm A and Arm C ledgers were generated as text, not computed over the output bytes, because a code sandbox was not active. That cause is Gemini’s account as relayed. It was not independently verified. The fact that those claimed digests are not the hashes of the received bytes was already visible, and it remains visible. The coordinator’s SHA-256 values over the received bytes are genuine. The numerical scores can be recomputed from those bytes. They describe agreement with the sealed grades. The admission does not declare the scientific content of the rows false, and it does not automatically exclude Arm B.

Claude’s Arm C output hashes match the received files. The original Arm A bytes those rows point at were not in the deposit, so they were not re-hashed. The times Claude wrote on the rows are later than the seal clock. The report leaves that as a caveat, not a finding of fraud.

Arm A’s pre-registered leadership-match figure, at least 60% on Russia, Australia, and the United States across at least three models, was never scored. The planned nulls have no result file. Kimi’s Arm C was not started. Grok, Codex, and Manus returned no Arm C. Gemini returned no extension. Those gaps are not failures, and they are not passes. The round was closed. No further run is part of the record.
